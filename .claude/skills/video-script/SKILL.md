# Video script workflow

Turn one idea into a short-video script that earns its first 3 seconds, confirms its promise by second 5, keeps a question open past 12 seconds, and delivers its value before any ask.

**Where the rules come from.** `productkind/marketing/storytelling-playbook/README.md` (sources: Sam Parr, Kallaway, Mino Lee, Caleb Ralston, Paddy Galloway, Alex Garcia, Anna Corinne Howard), its short-video guide in section 8, and `productkind/marketing/storytelling-playbook/storytelling-formats.md`. Read the formats file every time; read the playbook when you need a formula or the reasoning.

**Why this matters for us.** Our September validation videos averaged 3.4 to 4.0 seconds of watch time, with most viewers gone by 0:01 to 0:02, whatever the visuals (GIF, B-roll and stock cuts scored within 0.3 s of each other). The script, the first frame and the first line are the lever. See `productkind/marketing/storytelling-playbook/research/01-diagnosis.md` and `productkind/marketing/content/campaigns/2026-09-pm-technical-fluency-validation/tiktok-experiment-review-6-14-sept.md`.

## Inputs to establish first

Ask for anything missing in one short message, rather than guessing.

1. **Brand and account:** `little-parrot` or `productkind`.
2. **Real material:** the idea, the source (a course, an article, a real moment, a result, a learner question) and every fact the script may use. Kinga's own rough wording, if she has it, is the best raw material: keep her distinctive lines and smooth the rest. Never invent a moment, number, result or backstory to fill a beat (language-rules section 6).
3. **Treatment:** how it'll be made. Treatments combine: pick the **picture** and the **sound**.
   - Picture: **Kinga to camera**, **screen recording** (mid-action), **own B-roll**, or gifs/stock.
   - Sound: **narrated voiceover** (ElevenLabs, built in `productkind/video-generator`, where own screen recordings and footage go in as `clip()` sections alongside gifs and stills), **Kinga speaking**, or **text only** (Garcia's B-roll-plus-one-line topic test).
   - Ask what real footage or screens exist (which prototype, which app, which recording), because the visual hook is built from them. If none exist yet, say in Visual notes exactly what to record.
4. **The job** (Ralston): **reach** (new viewers), **community** (people who already follow us) or **conversion** (waitlist, course). One job per video.
5. **Platforms:** TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts.
6. **Target length.** Set by the format and our current watch time:

   | Format | Length |
   | --- | --- |
   | Hook tests, B-roll plus text, win, POV | 7 to 18 s |
   | Before and after | 15 to 30 s (Kallaway) |
   | Checklist, educational how-to, level comparison, myth vs reality | 20 to 40 s: one beat per item, and a new question every 8 to 10 s |
   | Hero's journey, personal learning, lesson from others, personal update, day in the life, challenge, goal journey | 25 to 45 s |

   While our average watch time is under 7 s, keep to the short end, and never go past 60 s. Longer is fine when the loop holds (Parr: "no such thing as too long, just too boring"). If the material needs more than the range, cut to the strongest items, or split it into a series ("part 2" is a payoff-named follow-ask).

## Step 1: check the idea

Before writing a word, write the **log line**: one sentence a stranger understands in one second (Galloway's glance test). Then check it:

- [ ] It leaves a question the viewer has to watch to answer (Galloway).
- [ ] It's about a costly problem or real desire our viewer has (Ralston).
- [ ] It fits a proven structure or format (Mino, Galloway, Garcia).
- [ ] It contains something specific only we'd say: a real moment, a number, a name (Howard, Mino).
- [ ] A regular follower and a total stranger would both get something from it (Galloway).

If the log line takes more than one sentence, or needs context to make sense, reshape the idea before going on.

## Step 2: choose a storytelling format

Pick one format from `storytelling-formats.md` using its **Choosing a format** table, and follow its beat table. Name the format in the script's frontmatter. Personal formats need real material for every beat; if a beat has none, ask for it or switch formats. Teaching content gets a teaching format (11 to 15), not a fake mystery (Mino: match the hook to the real content).

## Step 3: write the hooks

Write **at least 10 hook candidates** (Galloway), then pick **three to test** (Garcia: test hooks with trial reels or alternate posts, then scale the winner). The three should differ in hook type, not just wording, for example to camera, mid-action screen and result first (the TikTok review's A/B/C).

Each tested hook comes with **its own second line** (the confirm), written to join the body at the same point, because a different first line usually needs a different second line. Everything after the second line is shared by all three hooks.

Each hook has **three layers** (Garcia), planned together:

| Layer | Rules |
| --- | --- |
| **Text hook** (on screen, frame one) | Readable in under a second. Few, plain words; no all caps (Galloway). It complements the spoken line rather than repeating it word for word, and it has to make sense with the sound off: for a question hook, put the question or the stake on screen, not a fragment. |
| **Visual hook** (frame one) | Something the feed doesn't usually show: an unusual angle, an action, a distinct setting (Garcia). A "click to unpause" moment frozen just before the payoff, mid-action or mid-sentence, never someone smiling at the camera (Galloway). A real face or a real screen, mid-action. |
| **Spoken hook** (first words) | The first **five words** carry it, because that's all a viewer hears by second 2. Lead with the gap, a number, a contradiction, a stake, or the viewer's situation. |

**Hook ingredients** (combine two or three): the viewer's exact situation read back (Mino); a contradiction, two true things that shouldn't both be true (Galloway); a specific number, name or result (Mino, Ralston); stakes on a clock (Kallaway); the cost of not knowing (Galloway); a specific question the viewer wants answered (Kallaway); a named audience (Parr); a short stated time frame, "in 60 seconds" (Mino). The 15 fill-in formulas are in section 3 of the playbook.

**Hook don'ts:** empty teasers ("You won't believe this"); topic labels ("How to run your app"); scene-setting before the tension ("You built something with AI to test an idea."); statement intros ("In this video..."); leading with the lesson; a yes/no question with a guessable answer; anything the video doesn't deliver.

**Hooks and language-rules:** a contradiction is two plain, true statements, never "Not X, but Y" or the two-beat setup-payoff ("Sounds simple. It isn't."). No oversold outcomes, invented numbers, strawmen ("most people think...") or pseudo punchlines ("Here's the thing").

## Step 4: plan second by second

Fill in the plan before writing the full script (Garcia: plan seconds 1, 2 and 3 individually, then 3-second blocks up to 12).

| Time          | Shows | On-screen text | Says                 | Open question                  |
| ------------- | ----- | -------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| 0:00 to 0:01  |       |                |                      |                                |
| 0:01 to 0:02  |       |                |                      |                                |
| 0:02 to 0:03  |       |                |                      |                                |
| 0:03 to 0:06  |       |                | **Confirm the hook** |                                |
| 0:06 to 0:09  |       |                |                      |                                |
| 0:09 to 0:12  |       |                |                      | **A new question before 12 s** |
| then per beat |       |                |                      |                                |

Rules the plan must meet:

- **Confirm the hook by second 5.** The next sentence proves the hook wasn't bait: it restates or extends the hook's topic (Mino). Deliver the promise within 10 seconds, visually if possible (Galloway: 60 to 70% can leave in the first 10 seconds when the promise isn't met).
- **Least context first** (Galloway): "This is an FBI agent. He's got 24 hours to catch me, go." Drip-feed backstory later.
- **A new question before 12 seconds** (Garcia: 12 s average watch is "where good videos die").
- **A re-hook at every section end** (Kallaway's danger zones), never a sales line.
- **Visuals illustrate the words** at each beat. The TikTok review found our visuals didn't.

## Step 5: write the script

Follow the chosen format's beats and Kallaway's loop: stakes, big question, head fake, re-hook, repeated, then payoff.

**Illustrating vs inventing.** Making abstract items concrete is required (beats, not lists), and doing it isn't invention, as long as it's clearly an illustration:

- **Allowed:** a generic example framed as one ("say, your customers' email addresses"), a mock-up of an interface to show what something looks like, a clock showing 02:00 for "woken at 2am", general facts that are true for anyone.
- **Not allowed:** presenting a staged scene as something that happened to us, or inventing our own experiences, numbers, results, quotes or learner stories. In first-person formats, every "I" or "we" claim needs real material.
- When in doubt, frame it as "say..." or "imagine...", or ask Kinga for the real version.

**Write for the ear:**

- One point per sentence, mostly short. Vary the rhythm (Parr).
- Every sentence earns the next one (Parr's slippery slope).
- Link beats with "but" and "so", not "and then" (Parr).
- **Re-hook phrases:** "...which would have been great, except...", "But here's the problem with that.", "And that's when I realised...", "...which is exactly why..." (Kallaway).
- **Beats, not lists.** A list of abstractions ("whose data it holds, who's allowed in...") is a summary. Turn each item into something the viewer can see (Mino, Parr).
- **Make numbers visual** (Parr).
- **Objections** only when we know real viewers have them: "Now, I know you're probably thinking..." (Mino).
- **No conclusion language mid-video** ("at the end of the day"), and no "as I said last time" (Galloway).
- **No choppy transitions** ("anyway, let's get into it"): "the best intro doesn't feel like an intro" (Galloway).

**Deliver the value in the video.** Give the answer, the steps or the checklist. Don't describe a problem and then pitch a course instead of solving it; Ralston calls that value-edging and it costs trust. Every September validation script did this.

**The ask comes last, and there's only one spoken ask**, matched to the job. A save nudge for a checklist goes in the caption when the job ask is something else:

- **Reach:** a payoff-named follow-ask ("Follow for episode two: how to find the failed request behind an error"), or none.
- **Community:** a question about the viewer's own experience.
- **Conversion:** one sentence, after the value, pointing to the waitlist or course. It has to promise something the video didn't already give (the deeper version, the hands-on practice, the next step), and only what the product really offers; if you can't say that truthfully, use a follow-ask instead. Never read a URL out. **Comment-to-receive** on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube Shorts: links aren't clickable in captions or comments there, and our TikTok has no bio link, so the ask is "Comment on this and we'll send you [the link / the checklist]." Any comment counts. Never a "comment [WORD]" keyword prompt: we have no automation, so we send every link by hand, by DM. On LinkedIn, the link goes in the post body. A spoken example: "Comment on this video and we'll send you the waitlist link."
- **During a running experiment** that says "no CTA inside the video" (such as the TikTok review's next experiment), follow the experiment: no spoken ask. The ask goes in the caption.

**Voice:**

- Brand narration follows **productkind-tone**. Kinga to camera follows **personal-tone-of-voice** (spoken register, her asides).
- Every line passes **language-rules**. Run `python3 .claude/skills/language-rules/scripts/check-banned.py <script path>` before the critic.
- For talking to camera, add delivery notes from Mino: picture one friend who needs this, emphasise a word in each sentence, use your hands, and use good front lighting.

**Narrated scripts for the video generator:**

- Each paragraph of narration is **one line**, never hard-wrapped. A blank line between paragraphs becomes a take boundary in the narration pipeline (`productkind/video-generator/PLAN-narration-driven-video.md`).
- ElevenLabs v3 audio tags are allowed sparingly where the delivery needs them, in the house style: `[pause]`, `[long pause]`, `[curious]`.
- The hook's first words must be spoken from frame zero. Put any logo or mascot animation after second 3, never before the hook.

## Step 6: evaluation loop (run this every time)

The script is never shown to the user until an independent critic has gated it. The writer and the judge must be different.

1. **Draft** the full `script.md` (see Output).
2. **Critique.** Spawn the `video-script-critic` agent (Agent tool) and pass it the path to `script.md`, the brand, the treatment, the job, and the real material the script is built from (so it can check nothing was invented). Don't show the draft to the user yet.
3. **Read the verdict:**
   - **PASS** → show the user the script, the three hook variants and a short note on what the critic checked.
   - **NEEDS REVISION** → apply the revision brief and re-run the critic. Up to **3 rounds**.
4. **After 3 rounds**, if issues remain, show the best draft and name the unresolved items honestly.

Afterwards:

- Captions come from the **captions** skill, once the video is made.
- Narrated videos are built in `productkind/video-generator`. The **production-script** skill turns the approved script into the video definition and the visual brief for every beat; the visuals then come from motion clips, own footage, or the **video-gifs** and **stock-video** skills.
- After posting, record the real numbers next to the prediction (Galloway: judge against your own expectation).

## Self-check (before the critic)

1. The log line passes the glance test.
2. A format was chosen, and every beat is built on real material.
3. There are 10+ hook candidates and 3 different hook types to test.
4. Frame one works with the sound off.
5. The first five spoken words carry the gap, number, contradiction or stake.
6. The hook is confirmed by second 5.
7. There's a new open question before 12 seconds.
8. Every section end has a re-hook, not a sales line.
9. The value is delivered before any ask; there's one ask, matched to the job.
10. Every beat has a visual that illustrates the words.
11. The length is within target, and nothing boring survived the cut.
12. The script passes check-banned.py and reads aloud naturally.
