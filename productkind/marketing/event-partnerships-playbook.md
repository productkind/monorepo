# Event partnerships playbook

How Little Parrot gets visibility and credibility from conferences, meetups, buildathons and other tech events without paying cash. Built on [the October 2026 research](../ai-research/event-sponsorship-research-2026-10.md). Written 6 October 2026; revisit after the first three events have results.

## The strategy in five lines

1. **We join events as partners.** We don't buy a logo spot. We bring something useful to attendees (teaching and course access) and the organiser gives us reach in return.
2. **Every event has to leave something behind that we can measure.** Each event gets its own code, link and QR code, and the organiser gets a results report afterwards.
3. **Every event also leaves assets we keep:** photos, a recording, a partner listing and quotes from attendees. These build credibility on our site and in the next pitch long after the day is over.
4. **Lead with proof.** SheBuilds IWD (March 2026): free course access for participants brought 2,603 site visitors and 663 registrations. That number opens doors.
5. **Teach, never sell, from the stage.** Organisers and attendees both react badly to sales pitches. The workshop earns the trust; the code and QR code do the selling.

## 1. Choosing events

We can't go to everything, and travel is our only real cost. Score each opportunity before saying yes.

| Question | 2 points | 1 point | 0 points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Audience fit | Mostly non-technical women who want to build something | Women in tech in general, or mixed builders | Mostly developers or a general business audience |
| Language | Sessions in English | Mixed | Not in English |
| Organiser reach | Email list plus active social channels | Social channels only | Nothing beyond the event page |
| What we get to do | Workshop or talk | Mentor, judge or panel | Logo only |
| Cost to us | Online, or free to get there | One flight and one or two nights | Several days of travel |
| Lasting asset | Recording plus partner listing | Photos plus listing | Nothing reusable |

**Say yes at 8 or more.** At 5 to 7, offer the online version (a remote talk or course access only). Below 5, decline politely and keep the contact.

**Best value first:**

1. **Online buildathons and cohort programmes** (the SheBuilds model). No travel cost, and participants use the courses during the event.
2. **Women-in-tech and founder communities with a newsletter.** One email to a list does more than a logo wall.
3. **In-person conferences.** Most expensive for us, but they bring the most credibility (photos on stage, "as seen at" logos). Pick two or three a year with a strong fit.

## 2. What we offer (the menu)

Pitch one of three named packages so organisers get a clear, specific offer. A named package is easier for an organiser to say yes to than a loose list.

### Community Partner (online, no travel)

- An event code giving attendees free access to selected courses for a set period
- Little Parrot promotes the event: 2 posts from Kinga's LinkedIn and one Little Parrot Instagram post
- A ready-made blurb and graphic the organiser can paste into their own channels

### Education Partner (most events)

Everything in Community Partner, plus:

- A talk or workshop, in person or online
- 2 to 3 Open Wings scholarships (lifetime access) for attendees, chosen by the organiser
- A recap post after the event, tagging the organiser and speakers

### Programme Partner (buildathons and multi-week programmes)

Everything in Education Partner, plus:

- Courses mapped to the programme (for example, the courses participants should take before building day)
- Kinga or Thomas as mentor or judge
- A results report the organiser can use with their own sponsors

**What we never offer:** cash, attendee data handling, or discounts so deep they cheapen the subscription for existing paying users.

## 3. What we ask for in return

Ranked by value to us. Ask for the top five every time; the rest are nice to have.

1. **A talk or workshop slot**, listed with our name and Little Parrot's
2. **An email from the organiser to attendees** containing our code and link (before or after the event)
3. **Listed as Education Partner** (or Community Partner) on the event page and in announcements
4. **A dedicated post** introducing Little Parrot as a partner, and a speaker spotlight for the session
5. **Permission to film our session and use event photos** in our own marketing
6. A mention from the stage at opening or closing, with our QR code on screen
7. Our offer on the attendee perks or resources page
8. The organiser's logo and a quote from them that we can use ("Little Parrot ran a workshop at…")
9. Aggregate attendance numbers afterwards

**Never ask for the attendee list.** EU data protection rules (GDPR) make it hard for organisers to share it legally, and asking makes us look careless. The organiser sends our offer; people sign up with us directly.

**Low value, don't negotiate for it:** a logo on a wall of logos, swag-bag inserts, signage with nothing to scan.

## 4. Pitching

### Timing

- **Large conferences:** 6 to 12 months ahead. Their sponsor plans are set early.
- **Meetups, communities and buildathons:** 6 to 10 weeks ahead, so we make it into their promotion.
- **Under 4 weeks:** only if the organiser approached us.

### Rules for the first email

- Short: five or six sentences.
- No attachments and no links (they land in spam filters). Send the one-pager once they reply.
- Open with what attendees get, then the proof, then the ask.
- Name one package, not the whole menu.

### First email template

> Subject: Free AI building workshop for [event name] attendees
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I'm Kinga, co-founder of Little Parrot. We make short, self-paced courses that help non-technical women build their ideas with AI.
>
> I'd love to bring something to [event name]: a hands-on workshop on [topic matching their theme], plus free access to our courses for your attendees. When we did this with the SheBuilds buildathon in March, 663 participants signed up and 132 finished a full course.
>
> In return, we'd ask to be listed as an Education Partner and to have the offer included in one of your attendee emails. We cover our own travel.
>
> Would a 20-minute call next week work to see if it fits?
>
> Kinga

Follow up once after a week. If there's no answer after the second email, move on.

## 5. The confirmation email

Once the call goes well, send a confirmation email that both sides reply "agreed" to. No contract is needed at our size, but everything has to be written down. Cover:

- **Partner title** and where it appears
- **What we deliver**, with dates: session title, length, format, language; posts and their dates
- **What they deliver**, with dates: listing, posts, attendee email, stage mention, photos
- **The code:** what it unlocks, expiry date, redemption cap, who shares it and where
- **Costs:** "Little Parrot covers its own travel and accommodation"
- **Content rights:** we may film our session and use event photos; each side may use the other's logo for this partnership; we send any material using their name for approval (3 working days)
- **Data:** no attendee personal data is shared; the organiser sends us aggregate attendance numbers
- **If plans change:** what happens if the event is cancelled, moved or goes online

## 6. Running an event

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Agreement signed | Create the event code, UTM link and QR code (one set per event). Add the event to `calendar.md` and create a campaign folder in `content/campaigns/`. |
| 4 weeks before | Send the organiser our blurb, logo, speaker photo and bio. Post our first promotion. |
| 2 weeks before | Check the listing and attendee email went out. Post again. Prepare the workshop. |
| 1 week before | Final slides, with the QR code on the first and last slide. Print a few QR cards for in-person events. |
| On the day | Teach. Get photos and the recording. Collect 2 or 3 short attendee quotes (with permission). Talk to the organiser about next time. |
| Within 24 hours | Thank-you message to the organiser. Recap post tagging the organiser and speakers. |
| Within 3 days | Share photos and the recording link (if agreed) with the organiser. |
| After 14 days | Send the results report (below). Add the organiser's logo and quote to our credibility list. |

## 7. Measuring results

Track every event on its own: an event-specific code, a UTM link (`?utm_source=[event]&utm_medium=event&utm_campaign=[yyyy-mm-event]`) and a QR code pointing to that link.

**What we measure, at 14 and 60 days:**

- Code redemptions or registrations
- Course starts and course completions
- New paid subscribers (60 days)
- Followers gained on LinkedIn and Instagram during event week
- Assets collected: recording, photos, listing, quotes, a repeat invitation

**Benchmark to calibrate against:** SheBuilds IWD turned 2,603 visitors into 663 registrations (25%), and 132 of them finished a course. We have no in-person benchmark yet; set one after the first two in-person events.

**Results report to the organiser** (half a page, sent at 14 days): attendees who signed up with the code, courses started and finished, one or two attendee quotes, a thank-you, and an offer to do it again. This report is also our proof for the next pitch.

## 8. After three events, decide

Look at cost (days and money) against registrations, paid subscribers and assets for each event, and answer:

- Which format brought the most registrations per day spent: online, buildathon or in person?
- Did any event bring paid subscribers, or only free sign-ups?
- Which assets have we reused (recording, logo, quotes)?

Then do more of the format that worked and update this playbook.

## First run: Tech Women Summit Moldova (24 October 2026)

Package: **Education Partner**. The research findings are in [section 6 of the research](../ai-research/event-sponsorship-research-2026-10.md#6-tech-women-summit-moldova-24-october-2026).

**Settle this week:**

1. Is our workshop one of the paid workshops? What will attendees pay, and how many places are there?
2. Will it be clearly listed as an English-language session?
3. Our partner title, and how many attendees they expect.

**Ask for:** Education Partner listing on the unde.io event page and on screen between sessions; a partner introduction post and a speaker spotlight on LinkedIn and Instagram; a stage mention with our QR code; our offer in any message to attendees; workshop photos and permission to film.

**Offer:** the workshop with our own travel; an event code for attendees; 2 or 3 Open Wings scholarships (it fits their "Rebuilding the Ladder" theme and gives them something to announce); our own posts before and after.

**Watch:** sessions are mainly in Romanian and English proficiency is moderate, so expect fewer course sign-ups than the room size suggests. The lasting value here is likely the credibility assets (photos, partner listing, a European women-in-tech summit on our list), so make sure we come home with them.
