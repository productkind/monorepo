## Strengths to steal (3 to 5)

1. Turn the audience into the research subject before telling your story: "What was the last major purchase that you made? Quickly think about it." Everyone silently rehearses the method before hearing his example.
2. Build a domestic, slightly embarrassing story with real dialogue and a puzzle ("why that bed?") whose answer is the principle. Then contrast it with the obvious roadmap (cooling, eco, price) to show what the method finds that intuition misses.
3. Tell failures from famous products: "Huge failure." about Reels, and the "Super confusing" Close Friends prototype. Insider failure stories are the content this audience cannot get elsewhere.
4. Use a repeated chapter template (principle, story, AI angle) with full-screen chapter cards, so a list talk stays navigable.
5. Show, don't describe, the craft point: let the animation play and narrate the detail while it happens.

## Weaknesses to avoid (2 to 4)

1. A four-minute runway of welcome, two credential passes and product copy before the first idea. The self-deprecating "absolutely zero" line should have been the opening.
2. A thesis that is announced and then abandoned: "decision-making" frames the talk and the title, but the chapters teach discovery and polish. Pick the throughline your stories actually support.
3. Speed and filler: about 220 words per minute, almost no pauses, and heavy "like / actually / kind of". Key lines never get space to land.
4. An ending that apologises for itself. Hedging ("just one way", "10 other ones ... just as valid") in the last minute drains authority; end on the strongest story or a concrete Monday action instead.
