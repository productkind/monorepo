# Distribution channel strategy, August 2026

**Goal:** reach more people and guide them to Little Parrot or the newsletter, in service of revenue growth.
**Based on:** PostHog referral data for littleparrot.app (365 days), Substack traffic and subscriber data, Kinga's personal LinkedIn analytics, and the channel + content analytics reports for LinkedIn, TikTok, Instagram and YouTube Shorts (all in `productkind/little parrot/data-analytics/`).
**Written:** 6 August 2026. Review: November 2026.

---

## TL;DR

Effort and results are inverted. Roughly 80+ short videos a month across TikTok, Instagram and YouTube Shorts produced almost no measurable site traffic (TikTok ~11 visitors, Instagram ~36 visitors in a full year). Meanwhile Kinga's personal LinkedIn, recently paused, is the single biggest attributable driver of Little Parrot visitors, and one partner email to Geek Girls outperformed a year of Instagram threefold.

**Focus on: Kinga's personal LinkedIn, community/partner emails, and activating the 800-person Little Parrot mailing list, with SEO as the compounding bet. Demote short-form video to cheap repurposing and keep TikTok only as a testing lab.**

## 1. What the data says

| Channel | Unique visitors | Quality (pages/visitor) | Effort level today |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | ~574 | 2.7 | Medium, currently paused |
| Email (all) | ~426 | 4.2 warm / 1.4 cold | Low |
| Google organic | ~284 | 4.7 (highest intent) | Zero (passive) |
| Facebook | ~133 | 3.4 | Zero (untagged, origin unknown) |
| BetaList, Reddit, events, communities | ~230 combined | 1.4 to 2.6 | One-off bursts |
| Instagram | ~36 | 1.4 | High |
| Substack | ~25 | 1.2 | Medium |
| TikTok | ~11 | n/a | High |

Direct/unattributed traffic (~3,164 visitors) dwarfs everything, consistent with what we already know: paid users sharing free-course links is the real acquisition engine, and it is invisible in attribution.

### Five findings that drive the strategy

1. **Kinga's personal LinkedIn is the best asset and it is idling.** 65K impressions (+184% year on year), 2,023 followers (+61%), 61% out-of-network reach, and a verified on-target audience (product people and founders in Lisbon, Budapest, London). It already delivers more site visitors than every other social channel combined. The recent dip is purely a cadence problem: zero posts in the last week, impressions down 62%.

2. **Short-form video is reach without business value.** TikTok's 16.7K views converted to 8 followers and roughly 11 site visits, a view-to-visit rate of ~0.07%. LinkedIn converts impressions to site visits at roughly 0.9%, more than 10x better. YouTube Shorts is worse than neutral: it serves women-focused content to a 100% male audience. The earlier "35% effort on TikTok" recommendation is not supported by the referral data; TikTok delivers views, not people.

3. **Partner emails are the highest per-effort channel.** The single Geek Girls email (`email-k-gg`) drove 106 unique visitors, three times Instagram's annual total, to exactly the right audience. Community refs (PWIT, WiP, MTP) show near 1:1 qualified visitors. The AI Summit cluster (event + raffle + follow-up emails) worked as a full funnel. Proven, repeatable, and only run a handful of times so far.

4. **Substack, as currently run, is not a growth channel.** 1,155 sessions in a year, 125 subscribers, all free, and only ~25 visitors passed on to Little Parrot. A third of its annual traffic came from one external placement (info.pendo.io) and its best-converting source is Kinga's LinkedIn posts. The newsletter's job right now is nurture, not acquisition; it grows as a byproduct of LinkedIn and Little Parrot, not the other way round.

5. **Google organic is the highest-intent traffic** (4.7 pages/visitor) and the only channel that compounds without ongoing effort. Currently ~284 visitors a year on zero investment.

## 2. What the content data adds

### The commercial centre of gravity is Lovable/vibe-coding prompting

The "your first Lovable prompt" piece is the top performer on three platforms at once: the best LinkedIn document ever (372 impressions, 241 clicks, 67% engagement rate), the best TikTok post (934 views, 8 saves, highest watch time, 79% female audience), and a decent Instagram carousel. TikTok search confirms buyer intent: people find us through "lovable prompt", "best lovable prompt", "lovable design prompts", "vibe code markdown", even Etsy prompt-guide searches. This is the clearest content-market-fit signal in the dataset, and it maps directly to our courses.

### Four content pillars rank consistently across every platform

| Pillar | What it wins | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Practical how-to / templates | Saves, clicks, search discovery (buying intent) | Lovable prompt everywhere; Markdown prompts; "business ideas" 6 saves |
| Story-led AI explainers | Retention and likes (watch quality) | Mechanical Turk: top YouTube Short (570 views, 50% stayed), most-liked TikTok (12 likes), high Instagram reach |
| Reassurance ("you're not behind") | Reach and follower conversion | Best TikTok follower conversion (+2), second-best YouTube Short, 195 Instagram views |
| Founder/personal story | Likes, humanising, profile visits | "I left my job": 9 likes and a follow |

Consistent losers: vague motivational set-ups and quiet reflective pieces ("Grow your followers, or be honest?" got 0% stayed-to-watch on YouTube). The honest, understated register that works in LinkedIn prose does not survive a scroll feed without a hook.

### Format behaves oppositely on LinkedIn vs Instagram

Documents/carousels are LinkedIn's conversion engine (30 to 67% engagement rates) and Instagram's weakest format (carousels reached 18 to 35 accounts vs 130 to 165 for Reels). The same idea needs to be a document on LinkedIn and a Reel elsewhere. Conveniently, the high-effort format belongs to the highest-value channel.

### Execution beats topic: the hook decides the outcome

The cleanest experiment in the data: the "25% of the job" idea posted twice on YouTube with different edits scored 36 views / 2.4% stayed vs 99 views / 7% stayed. Same message, three times the result. Across all short-form, viewers leave at second 1 to 3. The first line/second is most of the work.

### Audiences consume, they don't applaud

Clicks and saves are strong everywhere while comments and reactions are near zero (241 clicks vs 5 reactions on the top LinkedIn post). Saves are the best purchase-intent proxy we have. The only posts that earned comments asked one direct question. Design save-worthy reference content, end with one specific question, and don't read low likes as failure.

### Audience fit is steerable by content, not fixed by platform

TikTok's audience swung from 57% male / 86% Hungary on early posts to 79% female and internationally spread on the Lovable-prompt post. Women-focused, practical build content pulls the right audience; generic AI explainers pull the generic tech crowd (which is all YouTube ever served).

## 3. The strategy

### Primary: Kinga's personal LinkedIn (~half of marketing effort)

- **Fixed cadence of 3 posts/week, non-negotiable.** Reach collapses within days of stopping; the recent dip is cadence, not audience quality.
- **Weekly content rotation built on the proven pillars:**
  1. One **how-to document/carousel** (conversion and saves), led by Lovable/vibe-coding prompting topics.
  2. One **story-led explainer video** (reach).
  3. One **reassurance or founder-story post** (connection and comments).
- **Every post carries a path** to Little Parrot or the newsletter: link in the first comment, refreshed featured section. 366 link clicks from 65K impressions is a conversion gap, not a reach gap.
- Every document ends with one specific question; reply to comments quickly.

### Second: partner emails and communities (the repeatable spike machine)

- **Systematise the Geek Girls play:** one warm, member-first email per month through a community partner's list (PWIT, WiP, MTP, Slack communities, and new communities we join). One email a month at Geek Girls conversion would roughly double attributable annual traffic on its own.
- **Repeat the AI Summit event playbook once a quarter:** presence + raffle/incentive + follow-up email sequence.

### Third: activate the 800-person Little Parrot mailing list

The owned list already exists: ~800 registered Little Parrot users (6x the Substack list), currently receiving only the monthly update email. Warm email readers browse at 4.2 pages/visit, nearly organic-search quality, and each monthly update already brings back 10 to 30 people. The gap is frequency and content type, not capture.

- **Shift from company-centred to learner-centred content.** The monthly update answers "what's new with Little Parrot"; the new emails answer "what can I do this week". The proven social pillars are portable: practical how-to (Lovable/prompting tips, templates) wins clicks and saves; reassurance wins reach. One short, useful idea per send, drawn from existing course and social content, so production cost stays near zero.
- **Add one practical email between monthly updates** (roughly fortnightly overall). One tip or template, one CTA back to a course. Keep the monthly update as is.
- **Segment into three groups:** active free users get momentum-framed nudges toward paid ("keep going"); paid users get the share-ask (they are the acquisition engine); dormant users get the single best piece of content as reactivation.
- **Put a forward/share ask in every practical email.** 800 warm people forwarding useful emails is the share loop running through our best-converting medium.
- **Tag every send** (`?ref=` per campaign) and watch click-to-site per send and free-to-paid conversions in the following weeks. Two or three sends will show whether the practical format beats the update format.
- **Sustainability rule:** fortnightly held for six months beats weekly for six weeks.
- Secondary: make the Substack newsletter the default catch for LinkedIn traffic that isn't ready to register for Little Parrot.

### Fourth: SEO as the compounding bet (small, steady effort)

- Google organic is the highest-intent traffic and the only channel that grows while we sleep.
- **The keyword list comes free from TikTok search:** "lovable prompt", "best lovable prompt", "lovable design prompts", "vibe code markdown", "women in ai". These should exist as pages/articles on littleparrot.app or productkind.com before anything else.

### Demote: short-form video to repurpose-only

- Stop optimising for TikTok/Instagram/Shorts as channels. If a video already exists for LinkedIn, cross-posting costs little, so keep posting, but cap the effort and stop producing video-first.
- **Drop YouTube Shorts entirely**; the 100% male audience mismatch is structural.
- **TikTok's residual role is a testing lab, not a channel:** the fastest, cheapest read on which hooks and topics resonate with women 25 to 34, plus a live feed of search demand. Judge it on what it teaches, not on its 8 followers.
- On Instagram, if posting at all: Reels only; carousels don't reach non-followers.

### Fix attribution (one afternoon, high payoff)

- Standardise on `utm_source` + `utm_campaign` for every shared link (PostHog reads them directly).
- Find and tag whatever generates the untagged Facebook traffic: 133 engaged visitors from an unknown source is the cheapest possible experiment. Identify it, then do more of it.

## 4. Revenue recommendations

- **The share loop is the growth engine, so invest in it directly.** Direct traffic dominance says sharing is working. Make the share-ask more prominent at high-emotion moments in the product (course completion, certificate, streak milestones) and tag those links so the loop finally becomes visible.
- **Lead promotion with the Lovable/vibe-coding prompting topic.** It is where buyer intent, best content performance, and the course catalogue all line up. The reassurance angle is the emotional wrapper that brings the right women in.
- **Judge every channel on registrations and paid conversions, not reach.** Set up a simple weekly funnel view in PostHog: source → visit → registration → paid. Steering on views is how 80 videos a month came to outrank one Geek Girls email in effort allocation.
