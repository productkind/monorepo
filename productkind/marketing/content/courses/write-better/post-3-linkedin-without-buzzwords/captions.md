## Instagram (Collab post with Kinga)

How to write a LinkedIn post with AI that sounds like you

Ask a chat assistant for "a LinkedIn post" and you get its best guru impression: a rocket, dramatic one-liners, "10x productivity gains", a stack of hashtags. It writes that way because it imitates the posts it has read, and you never told it whose voice to use.

So tell it. Part two of your prompt is tone and style: friendly, understated, sincere, simple wording, no exaggeration, five or six sentences, and no buzzwords like "game-changer". That alone gets you a post that sounds like a person, and still says almost nothing about what you actually did.

Part three is the context: the thing you did, the one detail only you would know, and why it was worth your time. Whatever you leave out, the model invents.

Request, tone and style, context and background. Use those same three parts on a Slack message you keep rewriting, or a product update.

And the shortcut, if you only try one thing: paste one of your own past posts in as a style sample. A chat assistant mirrors patterns, so give it yours instead of a random LinkedIn user's.

A lot of us end up not posting at all because of that second-guessing, and we've been there too. You shouldn't need a different voice to be visible in your industry. ## LinkedIn (productkind page)

Ask an AI assistant for "a LinkedIn post" and you get its best guru impression: a rocket, dramatic one-liners, "10x productivity gains", a stack of hashtags. It imitates the posts it has read, and nothing in your prompt said whose voice to use.

So say it. The repair is three parts, in this order:

1. The request. What you want written.
2. Tone and style. How it should sound: friendly, understated, sincere, simple wording, no exaggeration, five or six sentences, no buzzwords like "game-changer".
3. Context and background. What you did, one detail only you know, why it was worth your time.

Part two on its own gets you a post that sounds like a person and says almost nothing. Whatever you leave out, the model fills in.

And if you only try one thing: paste one or two of your own past posts in as a style sample. These models work by matching patterns, so your own writing teaches your voice faster than any list of adjectives.

That second-guessing ("too formal? too braggy?") stops a lot of us posting at all.
