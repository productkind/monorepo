# Follow one click through your product

**Runs:** about 90 seconds. **Search phrase:** "how does a web application
work". **Learning path step:** 1, how a web application works: frontend,
backend and database.

**Treatment:** narration over a screen recording of one flow in the Little
Parrot app, then the same flow drawn as five boxes.

| Beat | Narration | Visual | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook (0-4s) | "You can say what your product does. Can you say how it works?" | Text card | **You can say what your product does. Can you say how it works?** |
| The situation (4-16s) | "In design reviews you follow the discussion while it's about screens and users, and lose it when someone says the word service. Nobody taught you what the parts are called or what each one does." | Screen recording: a product screen, then a slide of engineering words (service, endpoint, cache, queue) | frontend · backend · database · API |
| The exercise (16-27s) | "So pick one flow in your own product, not the whole system. Someone signs up, or saves something. Then follow a single click through it and write down every part it touches." | Screen recording: clicking "save" in the Little Parrot app | Pick one flow. Follow one click. |
| What you find (27-52s) | "The click starts in the frontend, the part you can see. It sends a request to the backend, the code that decides what's allowed and what happens next. The backend writes to the database, where the data is stored. Sometimes it also calls an API, a way for our software to send a request to somebody else's software, like the payment provider. Then an answer travels back the same way, and the screen changes." | Five boxes drawn one at a time as each is named, arrow travelling through them and back | 1 frontend → 2 backend → 3 database → 4 API → 5 back to the screen |
| The payoff (52-73s) | "Once you've drawn it, you can point to the exact parts a change touches. Someone asks for a new field on the form, and you can see it touches three: the form, the check that decides whether it's valid, and the table it's stored in. So you can ask which of the three is the slow one, before anyone estimates." | The five-box drawing, with three boxes highlighted for a "new field" change | One new field, three parts changed |
| Honest bit (73-83s) | "Show your drawing to an engineer and expect to be wrong about a part or two. That's how you find out which ones, and you only have to learn them once." | Drawing with a correction pencilled in | Expect to get it wrong. Ask anyway. |
| CTA (83-90s) | "Step one of our learning path is this exercise, on your own product. It's not open yet, link's in the comments." | Waitlist page, learning path step 1 in frame | littleparrot.app/guides/technical-product-manager |

## Short cut for TikTok, Reels and Shorts (about 50 seconds)

Keep the hook, the exercise and "What you find", which is the teaching. Cut
"The situation" to one line and drop "Honest bit". End on the payoff plus
"Follow for the rest of these." Speak and show the URL, and pin it in a
comment.

## Production notes

- **Screen recording needed:** one save or signup flow in the Little Parrot
  app, plus the five boxes drawn on screen. Drawing them live as the narration
  names them is the whole visual, so don't pre-draw the diagram.
- **Every term is defined as it's spoken**, because a viewer who already knows
  what a backend is doesn't need this video, and one who doesn't will leave if
  the words arrive undefined.
- **No numbers in the narration that we haven't checked.** If Kinga wants to
  say how many goes the Little Parrot drawing took, use the real number or
  leave it out.
