# A screenshot is not a bug report

**Runs:** about 77 seconds. **Search phrase:** "how to write a bug report".
**Learning path step:** 4, bug triage.

**Treatment:** narration over a screen recording of a bug being reproduced in
the Little Parrot app, then the report being typed.

| Beat | Narration | Visual | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook (0-7s) | "A customer sends you an error message. You forward it to engineering with 'any idea what this is?' Then you wait." | Screen recording: a support message being forwarded, one screenshot attached | **"Any idea what this is?"** |
| Why it comes back (7-22s) | "It usually comes back as a question, because a screenshot doesn't say who it happened to, what they were doing, or whether it happens every time. So engineering's first job is asking you, and you're asking the customer, and nobody has started investigating yet." | Message thread bouncing back and forth, timestamps ticking | Messages back and forth before anyone looks at it |
| The move (22-39s) | "You can do the first step of the investigation yourself. Try to make it happen again on your own account. If it happens, you know it isn't one broken customer. If it doesn't, say so in the report, because that points at something specific to their account or browser." | Screen recording: the same steps repeated in Little Parrot, the error appearing | Try to make it happen again |
| The five lines (39-57s) | "Then send five lines instead of a screenshot. What you did, step by step. What you expected. What happened instead. Who it happens to, one customer or everyone you tried. And what you've already ruled out. The last line changes the reply you get, because it tells the engineer where you've already looked." | The five lines typed out one at a time in an issue | What I did · what I expected · what happened · who it affects · what I ruled out |
| The payoff (57-68s) | "None of that needs a log, or the code, or a debugger. Describing the problem accurately enough that an engineer can start on it is the part of the investigation that's yours." | The finished report next to the original screenshot | No logs, no code, just an accurate description |
| CTA (68-77s) | "Step four of our learning path is a full bug investigation, including the requests and the records the software leaves behind. Not open yet, link's in the comments." | Waitlist page, step 4 in frame | littleparrot.app/guides/technical-product-manager |

## Short cut for TikTok, Reels and Shorts (about 45 seconds)

Keep the hook, "The move" and "The five lines". Cut "Why it comes back" to one
line and drop "The payoff". The five lines are the save-worthy part, so hold
them on screen together for two seconds at the end.

## Production notes

- **Screen recording needed:** a real reproducible problem in the Little Parrot
  app, or one staged honestly in a test account. If it's staged, don't imply a
  customer reported it.
- **No real customer content on screen.** Rewrite the support message for the
  recording, and don't use a real name, email or account.
- **This is the strongest "try it tomorrow" video in the set**, so it's worth
  posting third rather than last.
