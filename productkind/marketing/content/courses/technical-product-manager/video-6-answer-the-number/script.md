# Waiting days for a number you need tomorrow

**Runs:** about 43 seconds. **Search phrase:** "SQL for product managers".
**Need it comes from:** answering simple product data questions without
waiting.

**Treatment:** narration over a screen recording of a short query being run
against a Little Parrot test database.

| Beat | Narration | Visual | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook (0-4s) | "You need a number for tomorrow's meeting. Analytics will have it next week." | Text card, then a request dropping into a queue | **Needed tomorrow. Ready next week.** |
| The moment (4-10s) | "It's never the hard questions either. How many people finished onboarding last month. How many came back." | The two questions appearing as two tickets, one per cut | The questions are one line long |
| Why it costs you (10-14s) | "You join the queue, and have the meeting without it." | A meeting slide with a blank where the number goes | Meeting happens anyway |
| The shift (14-18s) | "Four lines of SQL answers that one, and it reads almost like a sentence." | Screen recording: four lines typed, one per cut | Four lines |
| The result (18-23s) | "Count the users, from this table, who signed up last month. There's your number." | The query running, one number appearing | One number, ten seconds |
| The transformation (23-29s) | "The slower part is knowing what the number includes. Test accounts. People who never confirmed their email." | Two results side by side, one including test accounts | Same query, two different answers |
| The payoff (29-35s) | "Once you can check that yourself, you stop waiting, and you stop quoting numbers you can't defend." | The number, now with a note on what it counts | A number you can defend |
| Comment prompt (35-38s) | "How long does a simple number take where you work?" | The queue again | How long does yours take? |
| CTA (38-43s) | "We're building this for product managers who don't come from engineering. Link's in the comments." | Waitlist page | littleparrot.app/guides/technical-product-manager |

**Cut cadence:** nothing holds on screen for more than three seconds. The
Visual column lists every change in order, so a three-part visual is three
cuts, and the longest beats are the ones carrying the most of them.

**Channel swap:** on TikTok, Reels and Shorts, replace the last line with
"Follow for more of these" and speak the URL, then pin it in a comment. The
comment prompt stays on every channel, because it's what earns replies.

## Production notes

- **Screen recording needed:** a real query against a Little Parrot test
  database, typed at readable speed. Show one count, never a table of people.
- **Say "last month", not a date range in SQL syntax.** The real date filter is
  the ugliest part of a query and it loses people who could follow the rest.
