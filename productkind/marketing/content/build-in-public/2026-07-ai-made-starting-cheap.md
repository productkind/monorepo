## Instagram (Reels, Collab post with Kinga)

You don't need funding to start your business.

Starting a business used to mean hiring for every job outside your own skills. A developer to build it, a designer to make it look like something you'd trust, someone to write the words. If you couldn't pay for those, you waited.

That's what changed. You can do those jobs yourself now, with AI alongside you, well enough to put something real in front of people. So the sums work differently for a small business: it doesn't need a team, which means it doesn't need to be big enough to pay one. An idea that serves a few hundred people is worth starting.

To be honest, AI can do the jobs you couldn't do before. It can't make the right people find you, and that part is still slow. We're still working it out ourselves.

So, what are you building? Share it in the comments, we'd love to check it out. And if you have a friend who keeps saying her idea is too small, send it to her.

#womenwhobuild #womenfounders #womeninbusiness #buildinpublic #nocode

## LinkedIn (productkind page)

You don't need funding to start your business. Starting one used to mean hiring for every job outside your own skills, and that's the part AI changed.

A developer to build the thing, a designer to make it look like something people would trust, someone to write the words. If you couldn't pay for those, you waited, or you didn't start at all.

You can do those jobs yourself now, with AI alongside you. Not to a studio standard, but well enough that people can use what you made and tell you whether they want it. So the sums work differently for a small business: it doesn't need a team, which means it doesn't need to be big enough to pay one. An idea that works for a few hundred people, in one town or one profession, is worth starting.

To be honest, that's only half the story. AI can do the jobs you couldn't do before. It can't make the right people find you, and that part is still slow, unglamorous, and the bit we're still working out with Little Parrot.

So, what are you building? Tell us in the comments, we'd love to check it out.

**Kinga:** When I started in product, a first version meant a developer, a designer and months of waiting, so we only built things we thought could be big. Our own first version was one course and a very plain page, and that was enough to find out whether anyone wanted it.

**Thomas:** Worth adding what still costs money: hosting, a domain, payments, analytics. It's small, but it isn't zero, and most of it can wait until someone has actually paid you.
