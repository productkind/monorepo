# Carousel: 230 quotes from women building with AI

**Source material:** `productkind/ai-research/women-ai-building-research-2026-08.md` (§3.1 to §3.9, §4.2) and the waitlist page copy in `../landing-page-copy.md` **Brand:** Little Parrot **Channels:** LinkedIn (PDF document post), Instagram, TikTok (PNGs) **Destination:** `https://littleparrot.app/guides/building-apps-with-ai` **Date:** September 2026

**One takeaway:** what stops women after their first AI build is everything around the code, not the code, and the learning path we're building comes straight out of those problems.

**Story arc:** the finding that reframes everything, 230 quotes and almost none about code (slide 1), then the pain in the women's own words, one cluster per slide: the wall of GitHub and deploy (2), not being able to judge what the AI is doing (3), the money and the loop (4), doing it in the gaps and alone (5), being told it doesn't count (6), the turn, none of this stops once the first build works, and we built Little Parrot with Lovable, customers pay for it, and every step we teach is one we had to work out ourselves (7), the honest split, which half the Discord answers and which half the learning path answers (8), what you'd be able to do (9), the waitlist with a send-ask (10).

**Ten slides, not the usual seven to nine.** Five pain slides is what carrying the whole research picture costs, and each one is doing a job (a different cluster, a different woman). Nothing here is padding. If it needs to come down, the cut is slide 2 folded into slide 3, not a thinner turn or a thinner payoff.

**On covering all the clusters honestly.** The deck raises pain the learning path does not answer: loneliness, identity, the legitimacy penalty. Slide 8 is what keeps that honest, by splitting the two halves and naming what answers each. Loneliness and the legitimacy penalty take other women, which is the Little Parrot Discord, and which is also what the research asks for (§4.2, "Not another course. A person, and a room."). Publishing, fixing and charging take skills, which is the learning path. An earlier draft of this slide said a course cannot fix the first half and left it there, which was wrong: we run the Discord.

**Quote handling.** Every quote is verbatim from the research file, which holds the source link for each. They are marked "(verbatim research quote; banned-list exempt)" because they carry wording we would not write ourselves. Two decisions for you:

1. **Attribution.** The slides describe the speaker rather than name her ("a designer, publishing her first build"), including for women who published under their own names on Substack and Medium. Naming them is the more standard citation and arguably the more respectful one; describing them keeps real women's names off a promotional asset. Currently set to describe. Say if you want names.
2. **Slide 6's quote.** The sharper version in the research is "whenever I posted about my product, I ALWAYS received mean comments, saying it was another vibe-coded crap (lol)". It is stronger and it puts "crap" on a Little Parrot slide. The one specced below is the attack itself rather than the report of it, and is clean. Swap if you prefer the sharper one.

**Alt text (for the post):** A carousel drawn from our research on women building with AI, which collected around 230 quotes. It opens with the finding that almost none of those women say they can’t write code. Then five slides of what women actually said. GitHub, databases and deploying are where you get stuck: a designer publishing her first build said "I was just scared of clicking something wrong". It works and you still can't tell what it's doing: a woman in r/ClaudeAI said "The problem is I can't judge what it's doing". Going in circles costs money: one woman blew through 100 credits in less than a week, another was at 3 AM trying to convince an AI to fix the same bug for the dozenth time. There's no spare hour for this, and no one to ask when you're stuck: building in little pockets of time between naps, bedtime and family life, and one woman saying building alone has been empowering but also confusing, frustrating and honestly quite lonely. And then someone tells you it doesn't count: a reply to a woman announcing her solo game studio said "So you're just a vibe coder? I have no interest in games made by AI". Then the point of the deck: none of this stops once your first build works, and we built Little Parrot itself with Lovable, and customers pay for it, so every step the learning path teaches is one we had to work out ourselves. Feeling alone, and being told what you built doesn't count, both take a community. Publishing, fixing and charging take skills, which is what the learning path we're building teaches, and you can apply every step to your own app, covering making a change you can undo, checking what happens to a customer's data and money, fixing a bug with evidence, and seeing where people give up so you can test a price. It ends with us, a product manager and a software engineer, building the learning path out of exactly these problems, and the waitlist at LittleParrot.app/guides/building-apps-with-ai.
