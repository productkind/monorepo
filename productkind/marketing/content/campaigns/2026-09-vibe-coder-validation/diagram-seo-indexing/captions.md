**Caption:**

Why your website isn't showing up on Google, and how to check if your pages are indexed.

You can write the best page in your niche and still get nothing from Google, because a page that isn't in the index can't be found.

Being in the index doesn't mean Google will rank it though. It's more like getting through the front door: the page can show up at all.

The check itself takes a minute, once Google Search Console is set up for your site. That's a one-off, and it's free. Open URL Inspection, paste in an important URL, and look for the line "URL is on Google."

If it isn't there, these are the five things to check:

1. an accidental noindex tag,
2. robots.txt blocking the page,
3. incorrect canonical URLs,
4. missing internal links, or
5. the page missing from your sitemap.

If you built your site with AI, your AI agent can audit all five and list the affected URLs before changing anything. Then go back to Search Console and request indexing.

Save this for the next time a page gets no traffic, and send it to someone who's just launched.

**Kinga:**

One thing we do whenever we publish a new page: submit it in Google Search Console and in Bing Webmaster Tools. They're separate indexes, so you cover your bases by telling each one directly about your new page, instead of waiting for them to find the page on their own. We've seen new pages show up in both within a couple of days that way.

**Thomas:**

I'd check the canonical URLs first, because nothing looks broken. The page loads, it reads fine, and the whole time the canonical tag is telling Google the real version lives at a different URL, so Google indexes that one instead. Especially worth checking on any page you duplicated from another.
