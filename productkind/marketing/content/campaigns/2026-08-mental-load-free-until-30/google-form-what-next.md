# Google Form: the thing you keep meaning to do

Linked from the "Stuck on something you keep meaning to do?" section in
`../../../../little-parrot/assets/emails/2026-08-mental-load-course-announcement.html`.
Build the form first, then paste its short link into the email in place of
`https://forms.gle/REPLACE-WITH-FORM-ID`.

## Settings

- **Form title:** Stuck on something you keep meaning to do?
- **Form description:** We're deciding what to build next, and we'd rather hear what you're actually stuck on than give you a list of ideas to choose from.
- **Collect email addresses:** off. (Google's own setting makes it required and
  signs people in. The optional email question below does the job instead.)
- **Limit to one response:** off. No sign-in required.
- **Confirmation message:** Thank you. We read every answer. If you left your
  email, we'll come back to you when we build something for it.

## Question 1 (optional)

**What have you been meaning to do, but haven't worked out how?**

Type: Paragraph (long answer)

Helper text: Tell us what you're trying to do and where you get stuck. Maybe you don't know which tool to use, what the first step is, or how to make it work the way you want. A sentence or two is plenty.

## Question 2 (optional)

**Your email (optional)**

Type: Short answer, with response validation set to Text → Email address

Helper text: We’ll let you know if we build something based on your idea, and may follow up with a couple of questions about what you shared.

## Why this question

- **It asks what she's stuck on, not what we should teach.** "What should we
  teach you next?" puts her in the chair waiting to be taught, and asks her to
  guess our roadmap, which she has no basis to do. What she's been meaning to do
  is something only she can tell us. Turning it into a course is our part.
- **"Been meaning to" points at a real intention, not a wish.** She has already
  decided she wants this, and has not managed it. That is a problem with a date
  on it, unlike "if AI could do anything for you", which invites a fantasy.
- **The second half is the valuable half.** Where she gets stuck (which tool, the
  first step, tried once and gave up) is what a course has to open with. Without
  it we would know the topic and still guess the starting point.
- **It doesn't assume she already uses AI.** Anchoring on AI ("what did you last
  try to get AI to do?") returns nothing from the large part of the list who have
  barely started, which is the group a course is most useful to. The examples in
  the description carry the scope instead.
- **"A sentence or two" keeps the cost of answering low**, which is what gets a
  reply from someone reading on a phone with a baby on her.

## Reading the answers

Group by the blocker, not by the topic. Ten people naming different projects who
all stopped at the same step is one course; ten people naming the same project who
stopped in ten different places is not. Where several answers stop at the same
place, that step is the course's first challenge.
