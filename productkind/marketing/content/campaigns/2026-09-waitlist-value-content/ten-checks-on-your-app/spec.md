# Carousel: Ten things you can check in your app

**One takeaway:** She can find out today whether the app she built is ready for a customer, by running checks herself, without reading code. **Format:** 9 slides · 1080 x 1350 · little-parrot

## Slide 1 (cover)

Visual: Cover layout, headline with one .hl phrase, kicker, SWIPE, waving mascot bottom right.

- Kicker: FOR WOMEN WHO'VE ALREADY BUILT AN APP WITH AI
- Headline: Ten checks you can run on your app, without reading any code
- Kicker: SWIPE →

## Slide 2

Visual: Paired quote cards, gradient-topped and muted, with a small label above.

- Label: FROM OUR RESEARCH
- Card 1: The problem is I can't judge what it's doing.
- Card 2: I don't know if my app is secure, if there is junk code, if there are any bugs.
- Footnote: Two women in different communities said the same thing.

## Slide 3

Visual: Headline with one .hl phrase, body copy below, no device.

- Headline: Making an app and checking one are different skills.
- Copy: Almost everything we found teaches you how to build an app. And you can check an app you could never have written yourself.

## Slide 4

Visual: Gradient-topped card holding the action, copy below. Slides 4 to 6 share this shape.

- Label: CHECK 1
- Card: Sign up twice with the same email address.
- Copy: If it lets you, one person now has two accounts, and neither one holds all her data.

## Slide 5

Visual: Same card shape as slide 4.

- Label: CHECK 2
- Card: Ask for a password reset, then wait for the email.
- Copy: Reset emails often never arrive, and nothing in your app says so. The customer just can't get back in.

## Slide 6

Visual: Same card shape as slide 4.

- Label: CHECK 3
- Card: Sign out, then paste in the link to a page that needs an account.

## Alt text

A nine-slide Little Parrot carousel for women who have already built an app with AI. It opens with two quotes from our research about not being able to judge what the AI built, then says that making an app and checking one are different skills. Three slides each give one check she can run herself: sign up twice with the same email address, ask for a password reset and wait for the email, and sign out then open a page that needs an account. Each names what the result tells her. A later slide lists three of the seven remaining checks and says these don't replace a proper security audit. The last two slides ask which check she is least sure about and point to the free worksheet.
