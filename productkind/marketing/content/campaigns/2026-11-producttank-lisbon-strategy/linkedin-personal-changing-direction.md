We've changed direction at Little Parrot a couple of times this year, and sometimes I had a nagging question: is this right?

Little Parrot offers hands-on courses that enable people to build technical confidence without an engineering background, and we came to that after learning from our users through experiments.

Then last Thursday at ProductTank Lisbon, I asked our speaker, Andrew, who's a product strategy advisor, if he'd ever seen an early-stage startup change strategy too quickly.

His answer was no.

In his book, The Strategy Cycle, he even writes that crafting a strategy feels messier than it looks, with false starts and pivots along the way: "That's not you doing it wrong."

He sees a strategy as a series of bets. You can't be certain any of them will work, and there's no right answer you can know in advance. So you keep evaluating your bets and fine-tuning your strategy as you learn more.

He also contrasted strategy with plans. A plan tells you who's doing what by when, but it doesn't give you any insight into how you'll succeed.

How do you decide whether to change direction or keep going with something you're building?

Thank you to Andrew for the talk, and to my fellow co-organisers Michelle, Luis, Yuliya and Paulo. And a big thank you to the Sixt team, especially Caio and Ingmar, for hosting us!
