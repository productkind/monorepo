Our workshop for product managers last Friday got an overall rating of 4.7 out of 5. And most of the people who wrote comments asked us to change the same thing:

They wanted more time.

We had two hours at PTW Palace in Lisbon, as part of Portugal Tech Week. 19 people came – product managers, founders, consultants, executives and a doctor – and everyone ran a real open-source CRM in a development environment and looked at how it works under the surface.

Participants asked for "more time to dig into the application components", and for "commands for the command line, error types, further areas to look into on my own".

To be honest, I agree with them.

It's always challenging to manage time in a workshop, because every extra topic we add takes minutes away from people trying things themselves. One participant wrote that it was good "to start feeling comfortable searching and getting my own answers".

That appetite for more time to try things shows the need for our cohort-based course, How Your Product Works: Technical Foundations for Product Managers.

Over three weeks, you work in a real application: clone it from GitHub to your computer, use an AI agent to understand how it works, make a change, open a pull request, merge it, deploy it to a test environment, and more. And you have time to try each step yourself, with live office hours on Tuesdays to share experiences and ask questions, and async support in a Discord community during the week when you get stuck.

So why would a product manager open a pull request?

Because doing something hands-on is the best (and most fun) way to understand it. And from my own experience, and from talking with other product people, you get more out of AI when you know how to use GitHub and keep information (not just code!) there that your whole team can access.
