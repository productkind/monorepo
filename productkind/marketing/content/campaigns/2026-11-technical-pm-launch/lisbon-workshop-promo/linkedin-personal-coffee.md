When my co-founder Tamas and I work from coffee shops, I order a batch brew and he orders a latte. The black coffee is given to him every time. I can only assume it's because black coffee is a manly drink? 🤷🏽‍♀️

We all have unconscious biases, and LLMs can reproduce them too. Most recently, I've encountered this when it comes to who gets to do the technical work.

Claude Code knows I'm a product manager, and it knows I build software with it every week. Still, in our planning documents, it kept assigning the technical tasks to Tamas, who's a software engineer.

The most insulting part? It proposed that **"Tamas and a helper go round"** to help participants at a workshop I was organising.

A helper?!

So I asked it why. Its answer started with: **"Two reasons, and neither is a good one."**

It explained that it had assigned Tamas "every engineering-shaped task" based on our job titles, and had fallen back on that assumption even though I'd corrected it before and saved the correction to memory.

And the irony? These were the plans for our workshop teaching product managers how web apps work. 😅

At our workshop on **Friday 13 November**, the product managers will do the technical work themselves.

It's free and hands-on, at PTW Palace in Lisbon, as part of Portugal Tech Week.

You'll run an open-source CRM in a development environment and investigate why saving a record in the app fails. The next time a customer reports a bug, you'll know how to gather technical evidence, have more informed discussions with engineers, and go beyond reporting the problem to help your team decide what to do about it.

I'd especially love to see women product managers there, whatever coffee you order. ☕
