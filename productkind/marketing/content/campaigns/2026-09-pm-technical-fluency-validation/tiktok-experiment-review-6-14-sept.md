**@littleparrot.app · 29 posts · v2, with format labels confirmed by Kinga**

## The one-paragraph version

Watch time is flat across all three formats — GIF 4.00s, B-roll 3.84s, Stock 3.75s on 45–51 second videos — and every single post loses its audience at 0:01–0:02. That is the headline and it doesn't depend on format at all. But with the correct labels, one format effect _does_ appear: **GIF posts are the ones that get capped.** Nine of sixteen GIF posts were held at ~250 views, and in the 10–14 Sept window — once the account's distribution pool had tripled — GIF was the **only** format still getting stuck there. B-roll and Stock never were. Separately, **B-roll earns roughly twice the likes per view** of either other format. Meanwhile the audience being served is 68% male, 83% Hungarian, 40% aged 18–24, and 14,644 views produced **0 net followers and 1 share.**

### 2. Distribution cap: GIF is the one that gets throttled

Views cluster in two tight bands — ~225–265 and ~715–820 — with almost nothing between. The low band is TikTok holding a video back.

| Format | Held at ~250 overall | 6–9 Sept | **10–14 Sept** |
| ------ | -------------------- | -------- | -------------- |
| GIF    | **9 of 16**          | 5 of 7   | **4 of 9**     |
| B-roll | 2 of 6               | 2 of 4   | **0 of 2**     |
| Stock  | 1 of 6               | 1 of 1   | **0 of 5**     |

From 10 Sept the account's pool had tripled and both B-roll and Stock cleared it every single time. GIF still got capped on 4 of 9 posts. It isn't timing — 11 Sept 12:52 (GIF) got 779 while 12 Sept 13:02 (GIF) got 263.

The most likely mechanism is TikTok's unoriginal-content rule. Recycled GIFs and stock reaction clips are precisely what it limits. **This reverses what I told you in v1** — with the labels corrected, format does affect distribution, just not watch time.

## What works

**1. Your own footage, especially your own screen.** The 8 Sept post showing a real laptop with a real website: 14 likes on 549 views (2.6%), **3.4× the account average** and 20% of the window's total likes on 3.7% of its views. It's the only post in 29 that showed a viewer something concrete instead of describing a scenario.

**2. Original footage clears the distribution gate.** Zero of seven B-roll/Stock posts after 10 Sept were capped. That is the most reliable format finding here.

**3. Consistent daily posting.** The pool tripling from ~250 to ~750 is real and it's yours. Keep the cadence.

**4. The scripts.** "A stakeholder asked for something simple — just sync it with the CRM" is specific, true and recognisable. The writing is not the problem.

---

## What doesn't work

**1. 45–51 second videos.** The most expensive mistake in the set. At 3.8s average watch, completion is ~0.5%. Completion is the strongest input into whether TikTok gives a video a second, larger audience — so there is never a second audience. The same script at 12–15s would deliver roughly 25% average completion instead of 8%, from a pure edit. (Estimate, not a measurement — which is what the next run tests.)

**2. The first two seconds.** Drop-off is at 0:01–0:02 on all 20 posts with retention data. Frame one is a visual with a sentence of burned-in caption over it, and nothing on screen tells a scrolling viewer what they're about to get.

**3. GIFs as a primary format.** 16 of 29 posts, the worst distribution outcome and the second-worst like rate. Reusing them is capping you.

**4. The visual never illustrates the words** — a glacier for CRM integrations, an owl for a PM story, a sailboat for app updates.

**5. A waitlist CTA and a link comment on 29 of 29 posts.** A 100% promo ratio from an 11-follower account.

**6. No face.** None of the 29 shows either of you on camera.

**7. Account region = Hungary.** 83.4% of reach going to the wrong country.

**8. Three to four posts a day.** Each gets its own cold pool; you spend four ideas to learn nothing.

## Turning viewers into followers and traffic

14,644 views produced 14 profile views and 0 net followers. That is not a CTA problem. People follow for one of three reasons — they expect **more of a specific repeatable thing**, they want to know **how a story ends**, or they want **that person** in their feed. Right now the account offers none of the three: every video is a complete, self-contained, faceless tip.

### Fix what's actively working against you (this week, zero production cost)

1. **Drop the link comment.** Off-platform link-outs suppress reach and a link on every post from an 11-follower account reads as spam. Bio link only.
2. **Change the account region to Portugal** if the setting allows. Single highest-leverage change available, costs nothing.
3. **Post 18:00–21:00 UK time** to bias the pool toward UK/Ireland/US rather than Hungary.
4. **Cut to one post a day** so each gets a clean pool and each test is readable.

### Build a reason to follow

5. **Serialise.** Replace one-off tips with a named, numbered run with an open loop — _"Day 4 of running the app I built with Lovable"_, _"Week 2: the thing that broke."_ Serialised content is the most reliable follower driver on TikTok because it creates return intent. Each post should leave one question unanswered.
6. **Put a face in it.** People follow people. Your pinned intro is the only post on the profile that reads as two humans rather than a content account — and it's the only thing a viewer who _does_ tap through has to decide on.
7. **Show the work, not the lesson.** "Here's the bug, here's what I typed, here's what it did" beats "here's how to think about bugs." Your single best post is the proof.
8. **Answer comments with videos.** Once you get any real comment, a video reply is the cheapest reliably-performing format on TikTok and it compounds.

### Fix the path to the landing page

9. **Stop sending cold traffic to a waitlist.** "We're building a learning path, join the waitlist" asks a stranger who watched you for 3 seconds to bet on something that doesn't exist yet. Waitlists convert people who already trust you.
10. **Offer something they can have now.** A one-page _"12 things to test before you publish an AI-built app"_ checklist. Trade the email for the checklist, not for a waitlist place. Same list, ten times the conversion.
11. **Comment-to-DM instead of link-in-comment.** _"Comment TEST and I'll send you the checklist."_ This keeps traffic on-platform (which TikTok rewards), generates real comment volume — yours is currently exactly 1 per post, your own — and turns a cold click into a warm DM.
12. **One page, one action.** The bio link should go to a single page with the checklist offer, not to a homepage.

### Set expectations honestly

At ~750 views a post, even an excellent 1% follow rate is ~7 followers per post. An audience is not built by optimising CTAs at this scale — it's built by one video breaking out, and breakouts come from **completion rate, saves and shares**. So optimise for those three and treat follower growth as the output, not the target.

## Next experiment

**10 days · 1 post per day · 10 videos**

**Hold constant**

- **12–18 seconds.** One idea per video. No CTA inside the video.
- **Original footage only** — your camera or your screen. No GIFs, no stock, for the full run. You have enough evidence that reused assets get capped.
- Frame one is a **face or a screen showing the actual thing.**
- Caption: one line plus the question. Bio link only, no link comment.
- Same 8 pain points — the writing is fine.

**Vary — three hook types, rotating**

|  | Hook type | Example opening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Kinga to camera | _"You built an app with AI. Can you change one button without breaking it?"_ |
| **B** | Screen recording, mid-action | Screen already showing Lovable rewriting 40 files — _"I asked for one button."_ |
| **C** | Result first | _"This took 11 minutes and cost me four euros."_ |

**Judge on these, not views**

| Watched full video             | 0.5%     | **≥ 20%** |
| Saves + shares per 1,000 views | 0.7      | **≥ 5**   |
| Profile views per 1,000 views  | 1.2      | **≥ 10**  |
| Net followers over 10 days     | 0        | **≥ 25**  |
| Posts capped at ~250 views     | 12 of 29 | **0**     |

**In parallel, no production cost:** 15 minutes a day commenting as @littleparrot.app on women-in-product, women-in-tech and AI-builder creators. The For You graph learns from who you engage with, not only from what you post.

**What the result tells you.** If watch time clears 7s and completion clears 20% but the audience still comes back 68% male and 83% Hungarian, the problem is the account's geographic anchor and the answer is a fresh account created in-region. If the audience mix shifts, you've found the loop — and the next test is hook type A vs B vs C at volume.
