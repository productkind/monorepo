# You only know the screens

'So how does your product work?' You can demo every screen, and that's as far
as your answer goes.

Behind those screens is a service another team owns, three integrations, cloud
storage you've never seen, and a database somebody migrated last year.

So when the load test fails, you can't tell if it's your problem.

You know the frontend and the backend. Nobody explains the infrastructure
around them.

If you want to understand that about your own product, that's what we're
building a learning path for.

By the end you'll be able to map out your product: what it depends on, who
owns each part, where it slows down, and what breaks when one fails.

The waitlist link is in the comments. Sign up and we'll let you know when the
learning path opens.

Which part of your product would you struggle to explain?

## Channel swap

The CTA line works as written on LinkedIn (first comment) and YouTube Shorts
(pinned comment), where a link in a comment is clickable. On TikTok, pin the
link too, and also speak and show the URL, because the account has no bio link

or comments and the bio holds five slots for more than eight destinations. The
comment prompt is the last thing said on every channel.

## Production notes

- **Screen recording needed:** one flow in the Little Parrot app, then the map
  drawn live as the narration names each part. Don't pre-draw it, the drawing
  is the visual.
- **Payoff to protect:** the four parts of the map in "What changes", in that
  order: what it depends on, who owns each part, where it slows down, what
  breaks when one fails. Everything else can be trimmed for time, that can't.
- **Not a three-tier diagram.** Software product managers already know
  frontend, backend and database, and a video that explains those three
  insults them. The subject here is what sits around and underneath:
  infrastructure, environments, integrations and the parts other teams own.
- **The product depends on those systems, it isn't made of them.** An earlier
  draft said "your product is a service another team owns", which isn't a
  thing that can be true. "Behind those screens is" keeps it accurate and
  keeps the screens as the thing the viewer already knows.
- **Nothing is defined in the narration.** The words appear on screen and the
  map shows what they do.
- **The question in the hook is ours, not a quote.** It's how a stakeholder
  asks it, in the words a viewer would recognise, and no real person is
  quoted anywhere in the set.
