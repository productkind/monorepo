# Done, and your users still don't have it

Engineering says it's done. Your users still don't have it.

It's built, it's tested, and it's waiting behind another team's release.
Nobody told you that team ships once a month.

You've been telling your stakeholder it's coming this week. For three weeks.

Done meant finished in your part of the system. That isn't the same as live
for your users.

If you want to know where your feature actually is, we're building a learning
path for it.

By the end you'll be able to follow one change through review, testing,
environments and every team it waits on, and say exactly what's holding it
up.

The waitlist link is in the comments. Sign up and we'll let you know when the
learning path opens.

How often do you release new improvements for your users?

## Channel swap

The CTA line works as written on LinkedIn (first comment) and YouTube Shorts
(pinned comment), where a link in a comment is clickable. On TikTok, pin the
link too, and also speak and show the URL, because the account has no bio link
until 1,000 followers. On Instagram Reels, speak and show the URL instead of
saying "in the comments", because Instagram links aren't clickable in captions
or comments and the bio holds five slots for more than eight destinations. The
comment prompt is the last thing said on every channel.

## Production notes

- **Screen recording needed:** one real change in the Little Parrot repository
  as far as the live site. Fast sequence, not a walkthrough.
- **Payoff to protect:** the route in "What changes", in that order: review,
  testing, environments, the teams it waits on, and naming what's holding it.
  Everything else can be trimmed for time, that can't.
- **The dependency is the story, not the stages.** The point is that a change
  can be finished and still not reach users because it waits on other teams
  and environments. Don't name and define each stage on screen, show the route
  and mark where it stopped.
- **"Three weeks" is ours, not a quote.** It's the shape of the delay product
  managers described, in the words a viewer would use, and no real person is
  quoted anywhere in the set.
