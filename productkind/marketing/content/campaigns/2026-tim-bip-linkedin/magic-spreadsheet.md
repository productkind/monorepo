I wonder whether many internal vibe-coded company tools will end up like the magic spreadsheets I’ve seen so many times.

Let me explain.

Every small and medium-sized company I’ve worked in had a person who was the spreadsheet magician.

Usually, that person identified a process in the organisation that needed some sort of bookkeeping. Then they started building a spreadsheet, adding formulas, vibrant colours, and, of course, a good amount of VLOOKUP.

Eventually, the whole company started using it and updating it periodically, and the spreadsheet acquired an iconic name. One company I worked at literally had one called the “Beating Heart of the Company”.

Eventually, it became one of the most searched-for terms on Google Drive. In status meetings, you’d regularly hear:

“The X/Y/Z team hasn’t filled in the spreadsheet this week...”

All of this sounds like the success story of a digital product. After all, an entire organisation uses it every week.

If the spreadsheet were publicly available and other organisations paid for it, I’d probably say yes.

But there’s a fallacy here.

Everyone in the organisation liked the spreadsheet magician, including the leaders. And when the leaders said, “This is the process now”, everyone had to use it.

But 90% of the time, nobody really asked:

Is this the best way to solve the underlying problem?

There was only ever one solution. Nobody experimented with different ideas, and nobody interviewed the end user who had to spend 45 minutes every week updating numbers in the sheet.

Basically, no product management was involved. There was only the building and using part of the product lifecycle.

Now everyone can become a magician with a vibe coding tool.

And in some ways, that’s worse than a spreadsheet, because these tools can produce something far shinier than a spreadsheet with a pretty colour scheme.

I keep hearing stories like:

“Our CFO can vibe code this ROI calculator now.”

And immediately I start thinking about the unlucky employees who were never asked whether they needed it, but now have to enter random data into that software every week.

Every new technology that makes building easier creates an even greater need for a product mindset.

Unfortunately, that mindset doesn’t usually come with the technology. Batteries are not included.
