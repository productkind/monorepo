# Signal B: the YouTube Studio checks

The one demand signal in `../steps-to-find-your-niche.md` that can't be scripted, because it needs a signed-in channel. About 20 minutes.

**Studio changed in July 2026.** The tab is now **Trends** (it used to be called Research), and Google says the rollout is gradual, so labels may differ from what's written here. Look for the concept rather than the exact wording.

---

## Read this before you start

For `ai-your-life-admin-00` specifically, expect thin results, and that's informative rather than a failure. Google Trends already put `mental load` at **0.0 on YouTube search** against 16.4 on Google. If Studio also shows nothing, that's two independent sources agreeing that this course isn't found through video search, which settles it.

Two things limit what Studio can tell us:

- **Most of it is scoped to your audience.** "Top searches" is built from your own viewers and saves over the last 28 days. Our YouTube channel is small and was stopped, so audience-scoped cards will be sparse or empty. That's a fact about our channel, not about demand.
- **There's a volume floor.** Interest levels are based on videos watched more than 1,000 times a week over the last 28 days. A real but small niche can therefore show as nothing.

So a blank is ambiguous: it can mean no demand, or demand below the floor. Never record a blank as "no demand" on its own. It only counts as evidence when Trends or autocomplete agrees.

---

## Step 1: What people actually searched to find us

The most valuable thing in Studio, because it's first-party and true regardless of the niche question.

1. **Analytics**, then the **Reach** tab (in the newer layout this may sit under **Advanced mode**).
2. Set the date range to **the last 365 days**, not 28.
3. Find **Traffic source**, and open **YouTube search**.
4. Record every search term listed, with its impressions and views.

These are real phrases real people typed before landing on us. They beat any third-party estimate. The August strategy found the equivalent on TikTok (`lovable prompt`, `best lovable prompt`, `vibe code markdown`), and that list became the SEO keyword list. Do the same here.

Even a handful of terms is worth having. If the list is empty, note that and move on.

For each, record four things:

- **Audience interest**, on the very low to very high scale
- **Whether YouTube flags a content gap**, and which kind (no results, weak results, or outdated results)
- **Shorts or long-form**, which is what the results and the gap card imply
- **Top existing videos** it shows, and anything that surprises you

## Step 3: The four cards on the Trends tab

Before leaving, read the cards themselves rather than only the search box:

- **Top searches.** Your audience's searches over 28 days. Expect this to be thin for us.
- **Breakout videos.** Well-performing videos from similar creators. Useful for spotting a topic that's moving before it's obvious.
- **Recent videos.** Topics your audience watched lately, so an adjacency map rather than a demand measure.
- **Content gaps for Shorts.** Note that this card is Shorts-only. We stopped Shorts over the audience mismatch, so treat anything here as information about the format rather than a reason to restart it.

## Step 4: Switch the geography

The Trends tab filters by country. Run the priority 1 and 2 terms for **United Kingdom** and then **United States** separately.

Two reasons this matters here: `adhd mum` is a British spelling and `adhd mom` is the American one, so a single-region view will understate the cluster. And `mental load` performed in GB, US and worldwide alike on Google, so a divergence on YouTube would be worth knowing about.

## What would change the recommendation

The current call is that this course gets found on Google and not on video search. One result would overturn it: **`mental load` or `mental load list` showing medium or higher interest with a content gap flagged**. That would mean YouTube demand exists that Google Trends' YouTube property didn't capture, and long-form video would be back on the table.

Anything else, including a strong showing on the ADHD cluster, supports the current plan rather than changing it. The ADHD terms are worth cheap pages either way, and a good Studio reading there raises their priority without displacing `mental load`.
