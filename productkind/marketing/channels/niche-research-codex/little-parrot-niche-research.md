# Little Parrot Niche Research Workbook

## Research objective

Find one search-led niche that can support a self-serve Little Parrot digital product and a long-term content cluster.

The result must satisfy all of these conditions:

1. People repeatedly search for help with the problem.
2. Current results do not help them complete the job well.
3. Solving the problem has commercial value.
4. One self-serve course, learning path or toolkit can solve a meaningful group of the searches.
5. Little Parrot has a credible advantage because of the team's expertise.
6. The niche can support at least 30 useful search-led pieces.

This workbook starts with hypotheses. No demand, gap or monetisation score should be filled from intuition.

---

## Little Parrot's fixed business constraints

| Decision | Boundary for this research |
|---|---|
| Business model | Self-serve digital products; no service dependency |
| Product formats | Courses, learning paths, templates, checklists and toolkits |
| Brand audience | Non-technical and lightly technical women |
| Language | English |
| Initial geography | UK, US, Canada, Australia and English-speaking Western Europe, Singapore |
| Value | Help someone earn, save meaningful time, reduce delivery risk or create something previously outside her skills |
| Exclude | General AI news, inspiration without an applied outcome, entertainment and isolated questions solved by one short answer |

Women do not need to include “for women” in their searches. The search niche can be problem-led while the product experience, examples, community signals and brand remain designed for women.

---

## Little Parrot's expertise advantage

The candidate niche should use several of these assets rather than only one:

- Software and technical literacy.
- Product management.
- Product development from discovery through delivery, launch and iteration.
- AI-assisted work and critical evaluation of AI output.
- Lovable.
- Claude Code and AI coding agents.
- Education and learning design for non-technical adults.
- Existing first-party feedback from women learning to build with AI.
- An existing library of courses and toolkits that can be repackaged or extended.

Founder fit must not replace demand evidence, but it can make an otherwise attractive niche defensible.

# Five candidate markets

## Candidate 1: From Lovable prototype to production

### Niche statement

> Help non-technical people turn a rough Lovable prototype into a reliable product they can publish, operate and put in front of customers without a technical co-founder.

### Why it fits

- Little Parrot already teaches the first build, product definition, debugging, publishing, GitHub and business fundamentals.
- Existing learner feedback repeatedly mentions credits, unclear running costs, bugs, generic design, publishing, integrations and monetisation.
- The audience already spends money or credits on Lovable and related tools.

### Main risk

Lovable's official education and free creator content may satisfy much of the beginner demand. The gap must exist in the messy path from generated prototype to trustworthy product, not merely in learning the interface.

### Problem clusters

1. Define a realistic first version.
2. Stop uncontrolled changes and wasted credits.
3. Debug authentication, database and state problems.
4. Understand hosting, Cloud and running costs.
5. Add payments and transactional email.
6. Protect secrets and customer data.
7. Make the design feel deliberate rather than generated.
8. Test the complete user journey.
9. Connect GitHub and retain control of the code.
10. Publish with a domain, analytics, legal pages and discoverability.

### Possible self-serve product

**From Prototype to Production in Lovable**: a staged learning path with decision guides, launch checks, debugging workflows and reusable prompts.

### Product-gap question

Would someone need a coherent, maintained path through several connected tasks, or could official documentation and a handful of tutorials solve the job adequately?

## Candidate 2: Move from Lovable to Claude Code safely

### Niche statement

> Help non-technical and lightly technical AI builders move their Lovable project into GitHub and Claude Code so they can make larger changes, reduce credit use and keep control of their product.

### Why it fits

- It combines Little Parrot's Lovable, software, GitHub, AI-agent and teaching expertise.
- The transition contains setup, terminology and risk that individual tool tutorials may not explain as one journey.
- The audience has already experienced the limitations of an AI app builder and has a reason to seek more control.

### Main risk

The audience may be too small, too technically confident to buy beginner education, or reluctant to leave Lovable's interface. Tool behaviour also changes quickly, increasing maintenance cost.

### Problem clusters

1. Understand what moving to GitHub changes.
2. Decide when staying in Lovable is still better.
3. Connect or export a Lovable project to GitHub.
4. Install the local development prerequisites.
5. Run and preview the project locally.
6. Understand repository, branch, commit, pull and push.
7. Open the project in Claude Code.
8. Give Claude Code safe, bounded tasks.
9. Review changes before accepting them.
10. Recover from a bad change.
11. Keep secrets out of GitHub.
12. Work across Lovable, GitHub and Claude Code without losing changes.
13. Deploy after editing outside Lovable.
14. Estimate tool and model costs.
15. Decide when a developer is actually needed.

### Possible self-serve product

**Beyond Lovable**: a beginner-safe path from a working Lovable app to GitHub and Claude Code, with setup guides, safety checks and task recipes.

### Product-gap question

Is there a large group actively searching for the transition as one outcome, or only disconnected searches for individual setup steps?

## Candidate 3: Evidence-safe AI workflows for product managers

### Niche statement

> Help product managers use AI for recurring discovery and delivery work without losing evidence, context or judgement.

### Why it fits

- Product management and end-to-end product development are core team expertise.
- The audience has recurring professional tasks and established learning and software budgets.
- Little Parrot can teach evaluation and product judgement rather than generic prompting.
- Kinga's existing LinkedIn audience includes product people.

### Main risk

“AI for product managers” is already a crowded category. A viable niche must be narrower and tied to a repeated high-value workflow.

### Problem clusters

1. Prepare an interview guide from a research question.
2. Clean and organise transcripts.
3. Summarise interviews without erasing disagreement.
4. Trace insights back to source evidence.
5. Identify themes across interviews.
6. Separate observations from AI inference.
7. Turn research into opportunity statements.
8. Analyse support tickets and feedback at scale.
9. Compare competitors without inventing facts.
10. Draft product requirements with adequate context.
11. Turn fuzzy requests into testable requirements.
12. Create user stories and acceptance criteria.
13. Critique AI-generated specifications.
14. Prepare stakeholder updates from source material.
15. Explain technical concepts accurately to non-technical colleagues.
16. Analyse product data with AI safely.
17. Create decision records.
18. Prepare prioritisation inputs without outsourcing the decision.
19. Detect hallucinated evidence and unsupported claims.
20. Build a repeatable AI context system for product work.

### Possible self-serve product

Do not start with a general “AI for PMs” course. The research should identify the strongest sub-cluster, such as **AI Research Synthesis With Traceable Evidence** or **From Fuzzy Stakeholder Request to Testable Product Requirement**.

### Product-gap question

Which individual PM workflow has enough clustered demand and sufficiently poor answers to support its own product?

## Candidate 4: AI-built client assets for independent consultants

### Niche statement

> Help independent consultants turn their expertise into interactive lead magnets, assessments, calculators and lightweight client tools without hiring a developer.

### Why it fits

- The output has a direct commercial purpose: attracting, qualifying or serving clients.
- It combines product discovery, product design, Lovable, AI and go-to-market expertise.
- A self-serve product can include patterns, templates, prompts and build instructions.
- The audience may already pay for websites, email tools, business education and lead-generation products.

### Main risk

Search demand may be fragmented across “quiz”, “calculator”, “assessment”, “lead magnet”, “client portal” and tool-specific searches. The cluster may be commercially coherent even when no single umbrella phrase is large.

### Problem clusters

1. Choose a client problem suitable for an interactive asset.
2. Turn expertise into a useful diagnostic.
3. Design an assessment and scoring model.
4. Build a calculator without coding.
5. Create a personalised results page.
6. Capture leads with consent.
7. Connect the asset to an email platform.
8. Create a useful follow-up sequence with AI.
9. Qualify leads without creating a hostile form.
10. Make the asset match an existing brand.
11. Embed or link the asset from a website.
12. Measure completion and conversion.
13. Protect client and prospect data.
14. Test the asset before promotion.
15. Use the asset in LinkedIn content.
16. Turn a framework into a small client tool.
17. Build a client onboarding portal.
18. Automate a repetitive client hand-off.
19. Maintain an AI-built asset after launch.
20. Calculate whether the asset generates enough qualified work.

### Possible self-serve product

**Build an Interactive Lead Magnet With AI**: choose the right format, define the logic, build it, connect lead capture and measure whether it produces qualified enquiries.

### Product-gap question

Do consultants search for the outcome in meaningful volume, and do they prefer a self-serve build product over a template or done-for-you solution?

## Candidate 5: AI-assisted product validation for solo founders

### Niche statement

> Help solo founders use AI to test a product idea, gather evidence and choose a small first version before spending weeks building the wrong thing.

### Why it fits

- It uses product discovery, research, product strategy, AI and education expertise.
- It addresses a costly failure: building before validating the problem or route to customers.
- It can lead naturally into Little Parrot's app-building and launch products.
- The output can be a complete validation pack rather than general startup theory.

### Main risk

Founders often prefer building to research, may search for “business ideas” rather than rigorous validation, and are heavily targeted by existing startup content. Search volume may be high but commercially weak or poorly matched to the proposed method.

### Problem clusters

1. Turn a vague idea into a falsifiable problem hypothesis.
2. Define one realistic first customer.
3. Find people experiencing the problem.
4. Research existing alternatives.
5. Use AI without fabricating market evidence.
6. Prepare non-leading interview questions.
7. Recruit interview participants.
8. Analyse interview notes with traceable evidence.
9. Distinguish compliments from buying signals.
10. Identify the riskiest assumption.
11. Run a demand test before building.
12. Create a smoke-test landing page.
13. Choose a meaningful call to action.
14. Price-test a proposed product honestly.
15. Define a narrow minimum viable product.
16. Remove attractive but unproven features.
17. Decide whether to continue, change or stop.
18. Turn evidence into a build plan.
19. Use AI to accelerate research administration.
20. Keep a decision and evidence log.

### Possible self-serve product

**Validate Before You Build With AI**: a guided evidence workflow with interview scripts, synthesis prompts, a smoke-test template and explicit decision rules.

### Product-gap question

Is the audience searching for a rigorous workflow, or is most demand concentrated around quick idea generators and generic “validate your idea” content?

# Research sequence for Little Parrot

## Step 1: Mine first-party language

Sources:

- Course-completion feedback in `/Users/kingamagyar/Documents/productkind/little\ parrot/user-feedback/2026-08-19/little_parrot_course_feedback_20260819.csv`
- Course step response in `/Users/kingamagyar/Documents/productkind/little\ parrot/user-feedback/2026-08-19/little_parrot_course_step_response_20260819.csv`
- Waitlist “what do you want to learn?” answers: `/Users/kingamagyar/Documents/productkind/little\ parrot/user-feedback/2026-08-19/little_parrot_waitlist_what_do_you_want_to_learn.csv`
- Search Console queries.
- Little Parrot site search, if tracked.

Extract exact phrases describing:

- The desired result.
- The moment someone became stuck.
- The consequence of remaining stuck.
- Tools already tried.
- Costs, deadlines and existing paid subscriptions.
- What they asked to learn next.

Assign each phrase to one of the five candidates. Create a sixth candidate only if a repeated problem does not fit any of them.

## Step 2: Run the prepared seed list

The file `little-parrot-seeds.txt` contains 100 starting phrases: 20 for each candidate.

Run YouTube autocomplete for the UK and US:

```bash
../niche-research-tools/youtube-autocomplete.sh little-parrot-seeds.txt gb
../niche-research-tools/youtube-autocomplete.sh little-parrot-seeds.txt us
```

Save suggestions, not only suggestion counts. Add promising variants to the research sheet.

## Step 3: Run Google Keyword Planner

Import all 100 phrases. Use the same target regions for every candidate.

Export:

- Phrase.
- Average monthly searches.
- Three-month change.
- Year-on-year change.
- Competition.
- Low and high top-of-page bid.

Tag each row with its candidate and outcome cluster. Calculate total volume per cluster, taking care not to add obvious variants that represent the same searches without checking how Keyword Planner groups them.

## Step 4: Compare trends

Start with broad comparison terms as relative yardsticks:

```bash
python3 ../niche-research-tools/google-trends.py --geo GB --time "today 5-y" "lovable app" "claude code" "ai for product managers" "interactive lead magnet" "validate business idea"
```

Then compare phrases within each candidate. Run both Web Search and YouTube Search where relevant.

Do not infer market size from Trends alone. Reject a rising phrase only after checking whether its absolute Keyword Planner volume is also insignificant.

## Step 5: Audit supply for the top phrases

Select the six strongest demand phrases from each candidate: 30 phrases total.

Run the YouTube supply audit in small batches:

```bash
python3 ../niche-research-tools/youtube-supply-audit.py "lovable authentication not working" "lovable app launch checklist" "lovable running costs"
```

Manually inspect the top ten Google and YouTube results. Automated result counts cannot judge whether a result is exact, current, understandable or complete.

## Step 6: Check commercial supply

For each candidate, record:

- Paid courses.
- Template packs.
- Books and guides.
- Relevant software and its price.
- Advertisers on high-intent searches.
- Affiliate possibilities.
- Product reviews mentioning missing education.
- Forum and community comments asking for structured help.

Existing paid products are positive evidence when customers still express unmet needs.

## Step 7: Apply the product-gap gate

Answer for each candidate:

1. Can one self-serve product solve at least 15 related searches?
2. Does the job require a sequence rather than one answer?
3. Are mistakes expensive, frustrating or risky?
4. Would templates, worked examples and decision rules save meaningful time?
5. Does the content need maintaining as tools change?
6. Can Little Parrot demonstrate the outcome with real examples?

Reject candidates with fewer than four “yes” answers.

| Lovable to Claude Code | 4 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 71 | Unproven | Pass, 6/6 | Module/advanced product; not broad course |
| Evidence-safe AI for PMs | 3 | 2 | 5 | 5 | 70 | Unproven | Pass, 6/6 | Test only one precise workflow |
| AI client assets for consultants | 2 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 62 | Unproven | Pass, 6/6 | Park |
| AI product validation for solo founders | 5 | 1 | 3 | 5 | 66 | Unproven | Pass, 6/6 | Reject as search-gap niche |

Formula:

> Score = Demand × 6 + Gap × 6 + Money × 5 + Fit × 3

## Step 9: Run a three-cluster content and product test

For each of the top three candidates, create:

1. One complete how-to.
2. One template or checklist.
3. One troubleshooting or mistakes resource.
4. One product concept page with a visible intended price.

The product can be a paid pre-order, a very small paid toolkit or a price-qualified waitlist. It must remain self-serve; validation does not require a workshop or cohort.

Use separate campaign parameters and product identifiers for every candidate.

## Step 10: Choose on revenue evidence

Compare:

- Search impressions.
- Clicks and click-through rate.
- Engaged sessions.
- Registrations.
- Product-page visits.
- Checkout starts.
- Purchases or pre-orders.
- Revenue per 1,000 targeted visitors.
- Number of adjacent queries appearing in Search Console.
- Production and maintenance effort.

Choose the niche that produces the best combination of commercial conversion, search runway and product coherence. Do not choose on total social reach.
