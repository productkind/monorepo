# Steps to Find Your Niche

A lucrative niche is not simply “high search volume, low competition.” It is:

> **A specific audience with a recurring, valuable problem, actively searching for help, but receiving outdated, incomplete, poorly targeted, or low-quality answers.**

YouTube itself describes a **content gap** as a situation where viewers cannot find an exact match, or the available results are irrelevant, old, or low quality. ([Google Support][1])

Your research therefore needs to prove four things:

1. **Demand:** people repeatedly search for the problem.
2. **Supply gap:** the current results do not satisfy them.
3. **Commercial value:** solving the problem can lead to a purchase.
4. **Content runway:** you can make at least 30 useful videos around it.

Do not trust a single “keyword difficulty” number. Triangulate demand and then validate it by publishing.

---

# Step 1: Start with valuable audiences, not popular topics

Do not begin with “What is trending on YouTube?”

Begin with:

> **Which groups of people spend money to solve problems?**

Good starting markets tend to contain at least one of these:

* Professionals trying to earn more, save time, or do their jobs better.
* Businesses buying software, services, training, or equipment.
* Consumers making expensive or confusing purchasing decisions.
* Hobbyists who repeatedly buy tools, supplies, upgrades, or education.
* People seeking qualifications, career advancement, or specialized skills.

Use this niche statement:

> “I help **[specific person]** achieve **[valuable result]** using **[method/tool]**, particularly when **[constraint or situation]**.”

For example:

* Too broad: “Artificial intelligence”
* Better: “AI tools”
* Better again: “AI workflows for product managers”
* Strong micro-niche: “AI workflows for B2B product managers who need to produce PRDs, research summaries, and stakeholder updates faster”

Run every potential market through five questions:

1. Does this audience already pay for products, software, services, or education?
2. Is the problem urgent, recurring, expensive, frustrating, or career-related?
3. Would someone naturally search YouTube for a demonstration or explanation?
4. Can you offer something more specific than the existing creators?
5. Could you credibly produce 30–50 videos about it?

Reject markets where the only monetization idea is “eventually earn advertising revenue.”

---

# Step 2: Generate search phrases, not just niche names

A niche such as “home automation” is too abstract to evaluate. You need the actual phrases people might type into YouTube.

For each market, generate queries using these patterns:

| Search intent            | Query pattern                                               |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Learn                    | How to `[achieve outcome]`                                  |
| Fix                      | How to fix `[specific problem]`                             |
| Choose                   | Best `[product/tool]` for `[specific person]`               |
| Compare                  | `[Product A]` vs `[Product B]` for `[use case]`             |
| Implement                | `[Task]` template, checklist, tutorial, or workflow         |
| Avoid failure            | `[Task]` mistakes to avoid                                  |
| Work around a constraint | How to `[result]` without `[constraint]`                    |
| Specialize               | `[Tool/process]` for `[profession, industry, or situation]` |
| Update                   | How to use `[tool/version]` in 2026                         |
| Evaluate alternatives    | `[Product]` alternatives for `[specific audience]`          |

For example, from “AI workflows for product managers” you might generate:

* How to write a PRD with AI
* AI PRD template for B2B SaaS
* How to summarize user interviews with AI
* AI competitor analysis workflow for product managers
* How to create stakeholder updates with AI
* AI feature-prioritization workflow
* Prompt library for product managers
* AI product discovery workflow
* AI tools for nontechnical product managers
* How to evaluate AI-generated user stories

These are **hypotheses**, not yet validated opportunities.

Aim to collect at least **100 phrases across 5–10 potential markets**.

---

# Step 3: Prove that search demand exists

Use several independent demand signals.

## Signal A: YouTube autocomplete

Open YouTube in a logged-out or clean browser session and begin typing your phrase.

Look for:

* The complete phrase appearing in autocomplete.
* Multiple variations of the same problem.
* Modifiers such as “for beginners,” “template,” “example,” “2026,” “for small business,” or a particular profession.
* Adjacent questions that could become additional videos.

Autocomplete is a useful demand signal, but it does not tell you the actual search volume. A phrase appearing once is weaker evidence than a cluster of 10–20 related suggestions.

Try entering:

* The core phrase.
* The phrase followed by each letter of the alphabet.
* The phrase followed by “for,” “without,” “vs,” “template,” “review,” “example,” and “problem.”
* The problem first and the tool second, then reverse the order.

## Signal B: YouTube Studio Trends

In YouTube Studio, examine **Analytics → Trends**. Depending on your channel and available data, YouTube may show searches made by your audience or viewers across YouTube and may surface content gaps. ([Google Support][1])

Search your candidate phrases and record:

* Search interest or popularity level.
* Related searches.
* Whether YouTube flags a content gap.
* Popular existing videos.
* Whether interest appears concentrated in Shorts or long-form content.

If you already have a channel, go to the Reach analytics for your videos. The **Traffic source: YouTube Search** report can show the actual search terms through which viewers discovered your content. ([Google Support][2])

Those real search terms are often more valuable than third-party keyword estimates.

## Signal C: Google Trends

Use Google Trends to compare candidate phrases:

* Set the target country or region.
* Select **YouTube Search** as the search type.
* Examine both the past 12 months and the past five years.
* Compare related phrases against one another.
* Look at regional differences.
* Examine related and rising queries.

Prefer one of these patterns:

* Stable evergreen demand.
* Gradually rising demand.
* Predictable seasonal demand with commercial value.

Be careful with a phrase showing one enormous spike followed by almost nothing. That may be an event rather than a sustainable niche.

Google Trends data is normalized relative interest, generally presented on a 0–100 scale; it is not an absolute monthly search-volume figure. ([Google Support][3])

## Signal D: Existing video outliers

Search the phrase and look for videos where:

* The channel is relatively small.
* The video received substantially more views than the channel’s other videos.
* The video’s views appear large relative to its subscriber base.
* Several channels have successful videos about the same narrow question.

This suggests that the **topic**, rather than merely the creator’s reputation, is attracting viewers.

The strongest demand case has several signals at once:

> Autocomplete variations + stable or rising Trends interest + YouTube Studio evidence + successful topic outliers.

# Step 4: Perform a manual supply audit

Now search the exact query on YouTube and inspect the first 10–20 results.

Do not simply count how many results exist. YouTube may show loosely related videos, and search results can be personalized.

Create a spreadsheet with these columns:

| Field                | What to record                                  |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Search phrase        | Exact query being evaluated                     |
| Exact-match results  | Number of top-10 videos that directly answer it |
| Publication date     | Are the answers recent enough?                  |
| Views                | Approximate demand evidence                     |
| Channel size         | Is the topic accessible to smaller creators?    |
| Title relevance      | Does the title match the actual query?          |
| Content quality      | Complete, clear, credible, and actionable?      |
| Audience specificity | Does it address the exact person or use case?   |
| Comments             | What additional help are viewers requesting?    |
| Missing angle        | What could your video do better?                |

## Strong content-gap signals

Treat the following as heuristics, not universal laws:

* Fewer than three of the first 10 results precisely match the query.
* Results answer a broader question rather than the one searched.
* Most leading videos are outdated in a rapidly changing category.
* Videos receive views but have weak production, structure, explanation, or examples.
* The results target beginners when searchers need an advanced answer, or vice versa.
* Content is generic and does not address a particular profession, industry, country, device, budget, or use case.
* Search results are dominated by promotional vendor videos rather than independent explanations.
* Comments repeatedly ask for templates, examples, updates, comparisons, or follow-up tutorials.
* A modest channel ranks successfully, indicating that enormous channel authority is not essential.
* There are individual videos, but no channel or playlist comprehensively owns the subject.

A particularly attractive gap looks like this:

> People clearly search for the topic, one or two imperfect videos have accumulated meaningful views, viewers are still asking questions, and nobody has built a high-quality content cluster around the problem.

## Weak or misleading gap signals

Avoid concluding that a niche is attractive merely because:

* There are few results.
* Existing videos have few views.
* No major creator covers the topic.

That can mean there is no demand.

A genuine gap requires **demand evidence and supply weakness at the same time**.

# Step 5: Evaluate whether the audience is worth money

Do not ask only:

> “What is the YouTube advertising RPM?”

Ask:

> “What is one qualified viewer worth to the business?”

A commercially valuable viewer might:

* Buy software through an affiliate link.
* Download a paid template.
* Join a course or community.
* Request consulting, coaching, training, or implementation.
* Purchase equipment or supplies.
* Subscribe to a newsletter that later converts.
* Make the channel attractive to sponsors.

## Search phrases with stronger commercial intent

These frequently indicate that the viewer is closer to taking action:

* Best `[product]` for `[use case]`
* `[Product A]` vs `[Product B]`
* `[Product]` review
* `[Product]` alternatives
* `[Product]` pricing or cost
* How to set up `[product]`
* `[Professional task]` template
* `[Certification]` study plan
* How to fix `[expensive problem]`
* `[Software]` workflow for `[profession]`

Use Google Ads Keyword Planner as a secondary commercial signal. It provides estimates relating to searches and advertising costs for Google Search campaigns. Therefore, CPC and advertiser competition can indicate that businesses value the audience, but they should **not** be treated as exact YouTube search-volume or YouTube RPM data. ([Google Support][4])

## Score the monetization stack

Give the niche one point for every credible revenue path:

1. Relevant affiliate products.
2. Your own low-ticket product, such as a template or guide.
3. A course, membership, or premium educational product.
4. Consulting, coaching, implementation, or team training.
5. Sponsor categories with multiple potential advertisers.
6. YouTube advertising and Premium revenue.

A niche with four or five credible paths is generally more robust than one dependent solely on advertising.

Current YouTube thresholds also make this distinction important. In eligible regions, earlier YPP access to features such as fan funding and promoting your own products can begin at 500 subscribers, three public uploads in 90 days, and either 3,000 public long-form watch hours or 3 million Shorts views. Watch-page and Shorts-feed advertising generally require 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 public long-form watch hours or 10 million Shorts views. ([Google Support][5])

That is another reason to develop a product, affiliate, or lead-generation model instead of waiting for advertising.

# Step 6: Score every candidate niche

Use four scores from 1 to 5.

| Factor       | Weight | A score of 1                          | A score of 5                                                              |
| ------------ | -----: | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Demand       |    30% | Little evidence of searching          | Multiple query clusters, stable/rising interest, strong platform evidence |
| Supply gap   |    30% | Excellent recent results dominate     | Few exact answers, outdated or poorly targeted content                    |
| Monetization |    25% | Advertising is the only option        | Products, affiliates, sponsors, services, and recurring buyer value       |
| Creator fit  |    15% | Weak credibility or costly production | Strong expertise, differentiated angle, easy repeatable production        |

Calculate:

> **Opportunity Score = Demand × 6 + Gap × 6 + Monetization × 5 + Fit × 3**

Maximum score: 100.

Use this operational rule:

* **80–100:** strong candidate for immediate testing.
* **70–79:** worthwhile validation candidate.
* **60–69:** test only when you have a significant strategic advantage.
* **Below 60:** normally reject.

Do not let an enormous demand score compensate for terrible competition or weak monetization. All four factors matter.

# Step 7: Test the content runway

Before choosing a niche, perform the **30-video test**.

Try to generate 30 compelling titles in 20–30 minutes.

Use five problem categories:

1. Getting started.
2. Performing the core workflow.
3. Troubleshooting.
4. Comparing tools or methods.
5. Improving or advancing.

Apply six formats to each:

1. Tutorial.
2. Template or example.
3. Mistakes.
4. Comparison.
5. Case study.
6. Updated or advanced method.

Five categories multiplied by six formats gives you 30 potential videos.

Reject or broaden the niche when:

* You can only find five interesting questions.
* Every video would repeat the same answer.
* The niche depends on one temporary trend.
* You have no credible way to add new evidence, demonstrations, or experience.
* It is expensive or impractical to produce the necessary footage.

A strong niche is narrow enough for viewers to understand the channel immediately, but broad enough to support an ongoing publication schedule.

# Step 8: Run a controlled nine-video validation test

Do not spend six months building a channel before testing the hypothesis.

Choose your three highest-scoring micro-niches and publish:

> **Three niches × three search-focused videos = nine videos**

Keep the test controlled:

* Use comparable production quality.
* Use similar video lengths where the questions warrant it.
* Give each video an exact primary search query.
* Do not artificially boost one candidate with paid traffic or unrelated promotion.
* Give every niche a relevant conversion offer.
* Track links with separate UTM parameters or landing pages.

For a search-focused video:

### Title

Use the query naturally and add a concrete result.

Weak:

> AI for Product Managers

Stronger:

> How to Write a B2B SaaS PRD with AI: Complete Workflow and Template

### Thumbnail

Communicate the result or transformation. Do not merely repeat the entire title.

### Opening

Within the first 20–30 seconds:

* Confirm that the video addresses the exact problem.
* Show or describe the outcome.
* Establish why your method is credible.
* Remove unnecessary introductions.

### Content

Answer the basic query quickly, then provide examples, caveats, demonstrations, and next steps.

### Conversion

Match the CTA to the viewer’s current intent:

* Tutorial → template or checklist.
* Comparison → affiliate link or evaluation guide.
* Professional workflow → course, training, or consulting.
* Troubleshooting → diagnostic resource or service.

YouTube says its search system considers relevance, engagement, and quality. Relevance includes how the title, description, tags, and video content match the search; engagement signals can include watch time associated with the query. ([Google Support][6])

This means keyword research gets you an opportunity, but satisfying the searcher determines whether you keep ranking.

# Step 9: Measure the correct outcomes

Review each video after approximately 7, 28, and 90 days.

Track:

* Impressions from YouTube Search.
* Search terms that generated views.
* Click-through rate.
* Average view duration and average percentage viewed.
* Search-view growth over time.
* Subscribers generated.
* Comments and follow-up questions.
* Email sign-ups.
* Affiliate clicks and sales.
* Product or service enquiries.
* Production hours and cost.

Avoid choosing the niche based only on total views.

## Diagnose performance like a marketer

### Search impressions are strong, but CTR is weak

The opportunity may be real, but the title or thumbnail is not competitive.

**Action:** repackage the video before rejecting the niche.

### CTR is good, but viewers leave quickly

The promise is attractive, but the content is not satisfying the query.

**Action:** improve the opening, structure, proof, specificity, or production.

### Views are modest, but conversions are strong

You may have discovered a small, high-value niche.

**Action:** calculate revenue per qualified viewer before dismissing it.

### Views are strong, but no one clicks or buys

The niche may have attention but weak commercial intent.

**Action:** test a better-matched offer. If conversions remain absent, reduce its monetization score.

### Three or more exact-query videos receive almost no search impressions

The demand hypothesis may be wrong, particularly when autocomplete, Trends, and Studio data were also weak.

**Action:** reject or reposition the niche.

### Search impressions continue growing after the initial publication period

This is a strong signal that the video may become an evergreen search asset.

**Action:** publish an adjacent content cluster.

# Step 10: Compare niches using business metrics

Create your own equivalent of RPM:

> **Business Revenue per 1,000 Targeted Views**
> = `(affiliate revenue + product revenue + attributable service revenue) ÷ views × 1,000`

Also calculate:

> **Gross profit per production hour**
> = `(attributable revenue − variable production costs) ÷ production hours`

Example:

* Niche A generates 40,000 views and €200.
* Niche B generates 6,000 views and €900.
* Niche B also produces qualified email subscribers and consulting leads.

Niche B is probably the better business, even though it is the smaller YouTube audience.

Performance marketers optimize for **profitable customer attention**, not vanity traffic.

# Step 11: Build a cluster around the winning query

When one query performs, do not immediately jump to a different topic. Surround it.

Suppose this video wins:

> “How to Summarize User Interviews with AI”

The next cluster could be:

* Best AI user-research workflow for product managers
* AI user-interview summary template
* Mistakes when using AI for qualitative research
* How to remove bias from AI-generated research summaries
* AI research synthesis: three tools compared
* From interview transcript to product insights with AI
* Real product-discovery case study
* Advanced prompt for categorizing user pain points
* How to create an opportunity-solution tree from interviews
* Updated workflow after a major tool change

Create a playlist, link the videos to each other, and direct viewers toward a relevant template, email course, or product.

You are trying to become the most complete answer provider for a **problem cluster**, not merely rank one video.

# The most important traps to avoid

1. **Mistaking low supply for opportunity.** Sometimes nobody makes the content because nobody wants it.
2. **Choosing a broad demographic instead of a problem.** “Content for entrepreneurs” is not a search niche.
3. **Optimizing for views rather than buyer value.** A narrow professional audience can be extremely profitable.
4. **Depending exclusively on AdSense.** Build a monetization path from the first video.
5. **Choosing a niche that cannot support a series.** One good keyword does not make a channel.
6. **Believing keyword optimization can compensate for weak content.** Search relevance may produce the impression; viewer satisfaction determines whether the video continues receiving distribution.
7. **Testing only one video.** One title, thumbnail, or execution failure should not kill a viable niche.
8. **Publishing dozens of videos before measuring conversions.** Install tracking and a relevant offer from the beginning.
