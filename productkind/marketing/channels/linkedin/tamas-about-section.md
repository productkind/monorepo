## About

For five years I ran education at a coding bootcamp I co-founded. More than 3,000 of our graduates went on to work in tech.

Now I build courses at Little Parrot for people who don't want to become engineers at all: product managers and founders.

They need something different from a bootcamp. They need to understand how their product works to have more confident technical discussions with engineers and work more effectively with an AI agent.

In practice, that means understanding technical concepts, asking what could break, and judging whether a change is riskier than it sounds, whether an engineer or an AI agent suggested it.

I've been on the engineering side of those conversations for over 15 years, up to principal engineer, including at Datadog and Cloudera, and as the co-founder and CTO of a software development company.

In our courses, you build that understanding in a real app: running it in a development environment, using Git, the command line and a database, and knowing where AI agents do well and where they struggle. There's a cohort-based course and a self-paced one, both with live office hours where you can bring us your questions.

The first rule of thumb I'd give anyone: make one small change, test it, then move on to the next. When something breaks, you know which change caused it.

Since 2014 I've also mentored girls and women in IT, and at Little Parrot our mission is to help women build their own products, and their own income, with AI.

I post about the technical skills product managers and founders need when AI writes the code. Follow along, and tell me which skill you'd want to learn first.
