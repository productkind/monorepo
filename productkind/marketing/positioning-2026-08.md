# Little Parrot positioning, August 2026

**Question:** who exactly is Little Parrot for, how far beyond Lovable should the catalogue go, and where do paid users come from?
**Method:** `channels/steps-to-find-your-niche.md`, signals A, C and D. B (YouTube Studio) and Keyword Planner still need your logged-in accounts.
**Evidence:** 289 uniquely identified learners with recorded roles across `little-parrot/user-insights/`, the IWD and S02 funnel figures in `little-parrot-context.md`, `channel-strategy-2026-08.md`, plus a fresh Trends, autocomplete and supply run on 19 August 2026.
**Written:** 19 August 2026. Review: November 2026.

> Search queries and video titles are quoted verbatim as data. `check-banned.py` will flag some of them. Ignore those hits, they are what people type, not our sentences.

## TL;DR

Three things, in order of how much they will change the revenue number.

1. **The niche is a moment, not a job title.** No role owns even a fifth of the user base, and the roles all hit the same wall at the same point. Niche on the situation: *she has built something that works, and now it is stuck on her screen*.
2. **Keep Lovable as the front door, add a second pillar behind the paywall.** Vibe coding search interest is flat over the last twelve months. `ai automation` is roughly three times its size in GB, five times in the US, and grew four-fold within the year. But Lovable prompting is the only proven content-market fit we have, so this is an expansion, not a repositioning.
3. **Two paid subscribers is not a niche problem.** It is an offer problem. About 800 registered users converted 2 of them, and we have lost 5 of the 7 we ever had. Sharpening the audience fills a leaky bucket faster. Sections 4 and 5 are the ones that move revenue.

**Product management is the largest single cluster and it is three times the next one. It is still only 17%.** Nothing here is a majority. Picking one of these roles discards more than 80% of a user base that is already engaged and on-mission.

More useful than the split: the roles behave identically in the qualitative data. A judicial services manager, a recruiter, a startup advisor and a product manager all wrote the same three things. It worked. I did not know I could do this. What do I do now.

> "Which makes me feel a prisoner in my own business" (Giorgia)

The job title is incidental. The situation is the thing they share.

### Two positioning options the data kills

**"AI for product managers" is dead as a search position.** Google Trends, twelve months, with `vibe coding` as the yardstick:

| Term | GB mean | US mean | Direction |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| ai automation | 44.8 | 53.2 | 17.0 → 68.6 in GB, strongly up |
| vibe coding | 16.1 | 11.3 | flat |
| lovable pricing | 1.4 | 0.9 | up from zero |
| lovable credits | 0.3 | n/a | up from zero |
| ai for product managers | 0.1 | 0.7 | declining |

And the supply is entrenched. The first page for `ai for product managers` is Anthropic's own channel (57M views), HelloPM's 3.5-hour masterclass (429K), Aakash Gupta and Exponent. Low demand plus high-authority supply is the worst square on the grid in our own scoring model.

**The mission is not a search term.** YouTube autocomplete returned zero suggestions for `women in tech ai` and zero for `ai for women entrepreneurs`. This is the same finding as the mental-load research: the audience vocabulary is enormous, the audience-shaped *AI* phrasing is dead. Closing the gender gap is why we exist and it belongs in the About page, the partner emails and the community work. It cannot be the search position, because nobody types it.

### The recommendation

Use the niche statement from our own method:

> **We help a woman who has built her first working thing with AI get it in front of real people and keep it running, using Lovable and the AI workflows around it, when she has no developer, no budget for one, and about an hour a day.**

Role stays a targeting variable for channels and worked examples, not the brand. Kinga's LinkedIn audience is product people and founders, so lead there with product examples. Partner emails to Geek Girls or PWIT lead with whatever that community is.

This also names who we are **not** for, which a niche has to do: people who have not built anything yet (they are the free top of the funnel, not the paid product), and people who want to become developers.

## 2. How far beyond Lovable

The tension in the question is real, and the two datasets pull in opposite directions.

**Pulling towards expansion.** `ai automation` is three to five times the size of `vibe coding` and rising four-fold within twelve months, while vibe coding is flat. Our own learners asked for it unprompted: the top "what next" requests are publishing and monetising, then third-party integrations (GitHub, APIs, Kit, payments, ElevenLabs, n8n), then debugging and cost control.

**Pulling towards staying put.** `channel-strategy-2026-08.md` found that the Lovable prompting piece is the top performer on three platforms at once and the clearest content-market-fit signal in the whole dataset, with a 79% female audience on TikTok and buyer-intent search terms attached. That is the only proven fit we have.

**And a warning on the expansion.** The supply audit for `ai automation for small business` is twelve results of Liam Ottley, Dan Martell, Jason Wardrop and Alex Hormozi selling "build an AI automation agency and make $1M", with view counts up to 1,004,362. Enormous demand, entrenched supply, and a register that is the exact opposite of ours.

So the seam is not the head term. It is the audience nobody on that page is serving: someone who wants to automate **her own work**, not start an agency selling automations to other people. Every single top result offers a business model. None offers a skill.

### The split

- **Lovable stays the front door.** It is the acquisition engine, it is where the proven content fit is, and the ambassador who used our material word-for-word is evidence the teaching is better than the source. Free courses stay Lovable-shaped.
- **The second pillar sits behind the paywall and is framed as her own work, never as an agency business.** Automating her workflows, producing outputs that used to need a skill she does not have, connecting the tools she already pays for.
- **Do not rename the company around automation.** Vibe coding being flat is a twelve-month warning, not a today problem. At 2,600 visitors a campaign we do not need a bigger pond, we need a deeper one.

## 3. Where the money is actually leaking

The IWD funnel, from `little-parrot-context.md`:

| Stage | Count | Conversion |
|---|---:|---|
| Site visitors | 2,603 | |
| Registered | 663 | 25.4% |
| Started a course | 630 | 95% |
| Finished at least one course | 132 | 21% |
| **Paid** | **4** | **0.6% of registrations, 3% of completers** |

And the trajectory: 7 paid users ever, 4 in June 2026, 2 now. **We lose paid users faster than we add them.** About 800 people are on the registered list and 2 of them pay, which is 0.25% monetisation on warm, owned, on-target traffic.

Three diagnoses, all supported by our own data.

**We are the second bill in a stack, and the first bill hurts.** Credits are the single most repeated friction in the entire insights corpus, roughly 35 mentions, and the number one reason people stop finishing exercises.

> "credits credits credits!" (Marketa) · "I ran out of credits trying to follow along with the interactive parts." (seafoxxx) · "still don't know what it will cost though to publish, cloud, api etc." (Lauren)

A learner who has just been surprised by a Lovable bill is the worst possible person to ask for another €24 a month. We compete for the same wallet as the tool that produces the visible output, and we lose that comparison.

**A course library has no reason to renew.** Finish the courses, cancel the subscription. That is exactly the 7 → 4 → 2 shape. Nothing in the product creates a reason for month two.

**The ask lands after the emotion has passed.** The magic moment is documented and it is loud, and it happens at first build, not at course completion:

> "Its wonderful! A world that was impossible, just became possible!" (Kelly Neubauer) · "Feels like magic :D" (Ulrike P)

The very next thing they say is "now what": publishing, sharing, monetising is the number one ranked future-learning request across every source. We already own `lovable-publishing-00` and `launch-and-grow`. We are asking for money at the wrong minute.

## 4. What to change, in order

**1. Move the paywall to the publish moment.** Free covers idea to working app, which is the emotional peak and the share-loop fuel. Paid starts at "now get it in front of people": publishing, custom domain, legal pages, integrations, keeping it alive. This is the highest-intent minute in the whole product and the courses already exist.

**2. Make the subscription pay for itself in Lovable credits.** This is the sharpest asset we own and it is not being sold. Credits are the number one user pain, and the YouTube page for `lovable credits` is spam and one video titled "The scam that is happening at Lovable" (2,384 views), while the honest explainer sits at 236 views. We already have `vibe-coding-github-00`, which teaches editing without burning credits, and a `save-credits` campaign folder. A €24 subscription that saves €30 of Lovable spend is not a cost, it is a rebate, and it is the only pitch we have that survives contact with someone who just got a surprise bill.

Note the search pond here is small (`lovable credits` scores 0.3 against vibe coding's 16.1). That is fine. This is a conversion asset shown to people already inside the funnel, not a discovery asset.

**3. Sell a destination, not a library.** One price, one named outcome, a stated end point. People cancel a library the month they finish it. They do not cancel halfway to a destination they can name. Keep the subscription as the way to continue afterwards.

**4. Interview all seven.** We have had 7 paid users and lost 5. That is small enough to talk to every one of them this week, and no amount of keyword research will tell us what the 2 who stayed have in common or why the 5 left. This is the highest-value research available and it costs nothing. Do it before anything in section 5.

**5. Fix the ask itself.** 132 completers produced 4 payments and 18 LinkedIn shares. Momentum framing at the completion screen, and the share-ask at the same moment, since paid users sharing free-course links is the acquisition engine.

**6. Look hard at €24 a month.** It sits awkwardly next to a roughly $25 Lovable bill that the learner considers non-negotiable and considers ours optional. Worth testing an annual price, and worth testing the destination offer in point 3 as the anchor with the subscription priced below it.

## 7. How to validate each niche

### First, a correction to section 5

Section 5 originally said to judge the test on paid conversions. **At our volume that instrument cannot work**, and it would have wasted the experiment. Paid conversion currently runs at 4 in 663 registrations, and we have 2 live subscribers. To tell a candidate converting at 1% apart from one converting at 0.5%, each arm would need several hundred registrations, which is more traffic than a whole IWD campaign delivered. A six-week test judged on payments would return noise, and we would read the noise as an answer.

The fix is not a bigger test. It is a closer measure. **Put a real price in front of people and count who tries to pay it**, rather than waiting for the full funnel to produce a payment. A checkout attempt happens far more often than a completed payment, it means far more than a click, and it is readable at small numbers.

### The measurement spine, shared by both arms

Comparability is the whole point, so hold these constant and vary only the positioning.

- **One price, one page template, one CTA wording** across both arms. Different prices would test price, not niche.
- **A real, working checkout.** A page that states the price and leads to a real payment step. Count clicks to checkout, started checkouts, and completed payments separately. The middle number is the one that decides this.
- **Separate UTM per arm and per piece,** since PostHog reads them directly and the attribution gap is already a known problem.
- **The same three-week window for both.** Sequencing them makes them incomparable, because Kinga's LinkedIn cadence and any partner email land differently month to month.
- **One specific question at the end of each LinkedIn post.** Our own data says a direct question is the only thing that earns comments, and the comments are qualitative evidence the numbers cannot give.

**A confound to name rather than hide.** Candidate A can sell something that exists, candidate B can only sell a promise. That biases the comparison towards A. Keep both arms selling the same *shape* of thing, a named destination with a start date, and read B's numbers knowing they are a floor rather than a like-for-like.

### Candidate A: stuck after the first build

**The claim.** A woman with a working app will pay to get it in front of people and keep its running costs down.

**The sharpest instrument is one nobody else has: the list.** Segment the roughly 800 registered users down to everyone who completed Build Your First App With Lovable. That segment is warm, identified, and sits exactly on the hypothesis. One email to it is a better test than a month of posting, and `channel-strategy-2026-08.md` already shows why: a single Geek Girls email delivered 106 visitors, three times Instagram's annual total.

**What to send.** A named destination rather than a course list. Your app published, on your own domain, with the credit spend under control. Real price, real checkout.

**Supporting content.** The `lovable unlimited credits` page aimed at Google, and the same argument as a LinkedIn document, which is the format our data shows converts best there.

**Read it as validated when** the segment clicks through and a handful start checkout. At two live subscribers, five or six attempts is not a small result, it is a tripling of the business.

**Falsified when** the warmest and most on-hypothesis audience we own clicks the page but nobody starts checkout. That would mean the problem is real and our answer to it is not worth the money, which points at price or format rather than at the niche, and the next test should change the offer rather than the audience.

### Candidate B: automating her own work

**The claim.** The same women will pay to automate their own work, not only to build apps.

Nothing is built here, so this is a pure demand test and it should stay one. Do not build a course to find out whether anyone wants it.

**What to run.** A LinkedIn document describing one specific automation with a real before and after, pointing at a page that states the price and takes a waitlist deposit or a checkout for a first cohort date.

**The framing is itself under test.** Copy must describe her own work and never an agency. If the arm only pulls people once the agency framing appears, then the seam we identified does not exist for us, and the honest conclusion is that this category belongs to Ottley and the rest.

**Read it as validated when** people join at a stated price, and hold when the price becomes real rather than hypothetical.

**Falsified when** it draws reach but no signups, or when signups collapse at the moment the price appears. Reach without intent is the exact failure mode the channel strategy already documented for short-form video.

### Candidate C: closed, and what would reopen it

It is closed as a position on three signals, so it gets no arm. But naming the falsifier in advance keeps the decision honest rather than stubborn.

**Reopen it if** the single PM-specific piece (`how to create product mockups with ai`) outperforms both candidate A pieces on saves and clicks. That would be evidence the PM framing has life the keyword data missed, and it would earn a proper re-test rather than an assumption.

### Sequence

1. **Week 0, before any content.** The seven interviews, and segment the list. Both are free and both change what the arms should say.
2. **Weeks 1 to 3.** Run A and B in the same window, on the shared spine above.
3. **Week 4.** Read checkout attempts first, clicks second, reach last. Decide.

**Do not wait for statistical significance.** At this size it is unreachable, and treating it as the bar is how a small company talks itself out of acting on a real signal. The question is which arm produced more people trying to pay, and whether either produced enough to be worth building out.
