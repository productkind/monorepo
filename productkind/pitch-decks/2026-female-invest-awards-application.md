## Which award are you applying for?

The Builder

## What's your idea/business called?

Little Parrot

## In one sentence, what is it?

Little Parrot helps women with a business idea build the product themselves with AI, in short courses that fit around a busy life.

## What problem are you solving, and why does it matter?

Women with a business idea can now build the product themselves with AI app builders like Lovable. But building with them costs credits. Lovable's free plan gives you 5 credits a day, up to 30 a month, and a single prompt can use several. A custom domain needs a paid plan.

At Little Parrot we teach women to build their own products with AI, in short courses they can do on a commute or after the children are asleep. 933 learners have signed up and 160 have finished at least one course. The complaint we hear most is that they run out of credits halfway through a course. A learner who runs out has to wait for more credits, or pay for a subscription before she knows whether her idea works. We build Little Parrot with Lovable ourselves, so we run into the same problems our learners do.

Women-led businesses are underfunded, so a woman who can build the product herself, without raising money first, has a route to economic independence. We've both seen how access to technology can change a woman's career: I started mine in luxury fashion and have spent the past decade in tech, and my co-founder Tamas has mentored women and girls in software development for 11 years.

## What stage are you at?

Launched and generating revenue

## What's your vision? Don't hold back, we love big goals.

Our mission is to close the gender gap in AI adoption.

A gap like this has opened before, in computing. In 1984, the share of women studying computer science in the US stopped rising and then fell. NPR's Planet Money traced part of it to the first home computers, which were marketed mainly to boys. AI app builders now let anyone with an idea build software, and we don't want history to repeat itself.

Software is a form of power, and power should be shared. When a woman builds her own product, she earns her own income, solves problems her community has, and shows other women they can build too.

So we want Little Parrot to be where women learn to build with AI: short, practical courses in a kind space, where a woman can ask the questions she thinks are too basic.

We're building a profitable, purpose-driven business, and our Open Wings Scholarship already gives lifetime access to underrepresented groups in tech. This award would start a credit fund we run every year: five places this year, then more each year, and we'll ask the AI companies our learners build with to co-fund it.
