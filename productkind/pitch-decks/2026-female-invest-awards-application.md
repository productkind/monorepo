# Female Invest Women in Action Awards 2026: Little Parrot application

Applicant: Kinga Magyar

Deadline: 23:59 BST, 18 October 2026 (https://womeninaction.femaleinvest.com/)

---

## Which award are you applying for?

The Builder

## What's your idea/business called?

Little Parrot

## In one sentence, what is it?

Little Parrot's hands-on courses teach women with a business idea the technical skills to build and launch their own product with AI.

## What problem are you solving, and why does it matter?

AI can now write the code, but building a product still takes technical knowledge. In 230 quotes we gathered from women building with AI, they got stuck on the terminal, GitHub, databases and publishing. "The problem is I can't judge what it's doing," one wrote. So they struggle to fix, secure or launch what they've built, and the idea often stays a prototype.

Women-led businesses are underfunded: in 2024, all-female founding teams got 2.1% of US venture capital (PitchBook). So building the product herself, without raising money, gives a woman a route to economic independence, if she has the skills to finish it.

At Little Parrot, a woman learns these skills by building her own product, in short courses she can do after the children are asleep. Course by course, she plans her app, builds it, fixes it and publishes it. 933 learners have signed up and 160 have finished at least one course.

We've both seen how access to technology changes a woman's career. I started mine in luxury fashion, then spent a decade in tech, learning to code and becoming a lead product manager. I've been mentoring women, and my co-founder has mentored women and girls in software development for 11 years.

## What stage are you at?

Launched and generating revenue

## What's your vision? Don't hold back, we love big goals.

In 1984, 37% of computer science graduates in the US were women. By 2010, it was 18%. NPR's Planet Money traced part of the fall to the first home computers being marketed mainly to boys.

AI is opening a gap like this again. Across 18 studies of more than 140,000 people, Harvard Business School researchers found that women adopt AI tools at a 25% lower rate than men. We don't want history to repeat itself, so our mission is to close this gap.

Building software gives you power, and we want more women to have it. When a woman builds her own product, she earns her own income, solves problems her community has, and shows other women they can build too.

But it takes time to build a successful business. So our vision is an incubator that teaches women technical skills and funds them, giving them that time. Women who can afford it pay for their place, and their fees fund places for women who can't.

Today, we're a for-profit, purpose-driven business with self-paced courses where women learn technical skills and build their products with AI, and an Open Wings Scholarship that gives underrepresented groups in tech lifetime access.

By 2030, we want to fund 100 women a year.

## If you win, what will this money help you do?

This award would fund the first, smallest version of our incubator. Half of the £10,000 goes straight to five women who want to build their own product with AI but couldn't otherwise afford the tools. Each woman gets £1,000 for her AI tools and hosting her digital product, and lifetime access to Little Parrot through our Open Wings Scholarship.

We'll find them through trustworthy organisations that support women who need this, such as the Cherie Blair Foundation for Women and ReDI School. Each woman tells us what she wants to build, and we choose the five with the clearest problem, user and way to earn.

The other £5,000 goes on:

- £4,000 creating courses for Claude Code, Codex and GitHub Copilot.
- £1,000 setting up partnerships and the application page.

This first round tells us whether paying for the tools helps women launch, what else they need, and which partners reach them. We'll take those answers and the five launches to AI companies and partners, to fund a bigger round. The publicity from winning would also help with our No. 1 barrier to reaching profitability: reaching more people.

By the end of 2027, we want all five products live with their own customers.
