# Event sponsorship and partnership research (October 2026)

What a strong sponsorship or partnership looks like for Little Parrot, a small brand that can't pay cash, plus what to ask Tech Women Summit Moldova for (24 October 2026). Researched 6 October 2026. Items marked **[Inference]** are judgement, not sourced fact.

## Summary

- **Our offer is a partnership, not a sponsorship.** Paid sponsorship mostly buys logo and booth placement, which the research rates as low value for small brands. Established events already run a no-fee "community partner" tier that swaps attendee perks and promotion for visibility (GOTO Copenhagen, WomenTech Network). That is the model to copy.
- **Our strongest proof is our own.** SheBuilds IWD (March 2026) brought 2,603 site visitors and 663 registrations from free course access for participants. No published study comes close for our situation.
- **Ask for things that carry a link or a code**, not for logos alone: an organiser email, an MC mention with a QR code, a listing on the perks or resources page, a speaker spotlight post, recording rights.
- **Never ask for the attendee list.** EU GDPR makes it hard to share legally. Ask the organiser to send our offer on our behalf and collect our own sign-ups.
- **Track every event separately** (its own code, UTM link and QR code) and send the organiser a short results report. That report becomes our pitch for the next event.

## 1. What we can offer instead of cash

Events accept in-kind contributions but value them case by case, and some cap them (for example "no more than $5,000 equivalence") ([ASU](https://piper.asu.edu/node/212), [Everything Open](https://2027.everythingopen.au/sponsors/prospectus/)). Major League Hacking warns organisers about companies giving away "free credits or swag to avoid paying a sponsorship fee" ([MLH](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/understanding-your-sponsors)).

**[Inference]** Course access won't be valued at list price unless it clearly helps attendees. Frame it as a benefit for attendees, not a donation to the organiser.

Things we can offer, roughly in order of how much organisers value them:

1. **A talk or workshop** that teaches, not sells.
2. **Free or extended course access for attendees** through an event-specific code.
3. **Open Wings scholarships** (lifetime access) for a few attendees, which gives the organiser something to announce.
4. **Promotion of the event to our audience** (LinkedIn, Instagram, newsletter).
5. **Paying our own travel and accommodation.** Worth stating, as it saves the organiser a speaker cost.
6. **A mentor or judge role** at hackathons and buildathons.

## 2. What to ask for in return

Standard sponsor deliverables: logo (size and placement), social posts (count, platforms, tags), email inclusions (sends, position, timing), tickets, booth or table, speaking slot, stage mentions, and a post-event report with photos and numbers ([Remo](https://remo.co/blog/event-sponsorship-agreement-template), [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/sponsorship-agreement-example-key-clauses-to-include/)).

Sponsors increasingly question the return on signage and want "customer leads and engagement time with attendees" ([PCMA](https://www.pcma.org/convene-survey-event-sponsorships/)). "A logo on its own can't generate leads" ([Sponsorship Collective](https://sponsorshipcollective.com/?p=231733)).

**[Inference] High value for us:**

- A talk or workshop slot
- An organiser email or newsletter mention with our link or code
- Our offer on the attendee perks or resources page
- An MC mention pointing to a specific link or QR code
- A dedicated partner or speaker spotlight post on the organiser's channels
- Rights to use photos and the recording of our session
- A post-event message to attendees, sent by the organiser

**[Inference] Low value for us:** a logo on a wall of logos, swag-bag inserts, signage with nothing to act on.

### GDPR and attendee data

Valid consent has to name any third party relying on it, can't be pre-ticked and can't be bundled into registration ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/consent/what-is-valid-consent/)). EU attendees must opt in to sponsor emails through a separate checkbox ([Splash](https://splashthat.com/blog/gdpr-for-event-marketing)). **[Inference]** Don't ask for the list. Ask the organiser to send our offer, and let course sign-up collect our own consent.

## 3. Getting a return from a small event

- **Tracking:** a UTM link per event, an event-specific coupon code and a QR code to an event landing page ([Attendstar](https://www.attendstar.com/how-to-use-utm-codes-in-your-event-marketing-to-boost-ticket-sales/), [atVenu](https://www.atvenu.com/post/how-to-turn-qr-code-promotions-into-real-revenue-and-sponsor-value)).
- **Without a booth:** co-host a small meetup or dinner with other startups, create content on the event's theme, run LinkedIn posts around it ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/dear-saastr-weve-only-raised-2m-should-we-sponsor-events-like-saastr-annual)).
- **Follow-up timing:** thank-you within 24 hours, photos and recordings by day 3 ([i4a](https://www.i4a.com/blog/post-event-follow-up/)).
- **[Inference]** Send the organiser a short results report (redemptions, sign-ups, course starts) within two weeks. It shows we're a partner worth inviting back and gives us numbers for the next pitch.

## 4. The one-page confirmation

A sponsorship agreement should cover dated, specific deliverables; brand and logo use with an approval window (for example 3 working days); who owns content created at the event; cancellation and postponement; and both sides' responsibilities ([Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/what-is-a-sponsorship-agreement/), [Remo](https://remo.co/blog/event-sponsorship-agreement-template), [Sheffield Digital](https://sheffield.digital/eight-rules-for-securing-meetup-sponsorship/)).

**[Inference]** For our size, a confirmation email both sides reply "agreed" to is enough. Lines specific to us:

- Partner title we're listed under
- Coupon code, what it unlocks, expiry date and redemption cap
- Who shares the code, where, and by what date
- Travel and accommodation paid by Little Parrot
- Our right to record and reuse our session, and use event photos
- No attendee personal data transferred; organiser shares aggregate attendance numbers afterwards
- What happens if the event is cancelled or moved

## 5. Pitching organisers

- **What organisers weigh:** fit with attendees, who else is involved, value of the package, and the credibility of the person approaching them ([MLH](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/understanding-your-sponsors)). They want specific offers with clear deliverables, not vague proposals ([Sheffield Digital](https://sheffield.digital/eight-rules-for-securing-meetup-sponsorship/)).
- **Timing:** large conferences 12+ months ahead (corporate budgets are annual), never under 4 weeks ([Tickera](https://blog.tickera.com/how-to-get-sponsors-for-your-event/)). **[Inference]** Partnerships with meetups and buildathons need less, but 6 to 10 weeks leaves room to be included in the organiser's comms.
- **Mistakes to avoid:**
  - Trying to buy speaking time, or giving a sales pitch from the stage ([WordPress Community](https://make.wordpress.org/community/2014/05/07/meetup-sponsorships-and-other-local-community-stuff/))
  - Long first emails
  - Links or attachments in the first email; send the deck once they reply ([MLH](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/sponsorship-prospectus))

## 6. Tech Women Summit Moldova (24 October 2026)

**Correct name:** Tech Women Summit, organised by Tech Women Moldova.

### The event

- **When and where:** Saturday 24 October, 10:30 to 16:00, Tekwill, Chișinău ([unde.io](https://unde.io/events/1955-tech-women-summit), [diez.md](https://diez.md/2026/09/25/vrei-sa-faci-o-cariera-in-it-vezi-ce-poti-afla-la-tech-women-summit-2026/)).
- **Format:** the main stage is free with registration. Workshops are separate and **paid**, with prices still "to be announced".
- **Theme:** "Rebuilding the Ladder": how AI changes entry-level tech work, proof of work beyond diplomas, career switchers and returners, mentorship. This fits a building-with-AI workshop closely.
- **Audience:** students, people early in their IT careers and people considering a move into IT.
- **Language:** mainly Romanian, with some English content.
- **Size:** 2026 attendance not published ("80+ interested" on the ticketing page on 6 October). The 2022 edition had 300+ attendees ([UN Moldova](https://moldova.un.org/en/204954-tech-women-summit-platform-dedicated-women-any-age-who-work-it-or-want-pursue-it-careers)).
- **Organiser:** Tech Women Moldova, a non-profit community started in 2018 under Tekwill and sitting under ATIC, the national ICT companies association ([ICT.md](https://ict.md/call-for-tech-women-ambassadors-batch-2021)).

### Sponsors and reach

- **No published sponsor tiers.** The website is a holding page ([techwomen.md](https://techwomen.md/)).
- **Endava** is promoted as 2026 "Annual Partner". In 2022, partners included the Swedish government, EU Delegation, UN Women, UNDP, Pentalog, Stefanini and Amdaris.
- **How sponsors are shown:** dedicated LinkedIn thank-you posts (as for [Amdaris](https://cy.linkedin.com/posts/tech-women-moldova_techwomen-techwomenmoldova-techwomensummit22-activity-6983487288668295168-VqRe)) and partner labels. No evidence of logo walls or booths.
- **Channels:** LinkedIn 4,377 followers ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/tech-women-moldova)), Instagram 2,371 ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/techwomen.moldova/)), Facebook (count not found). No newsletter found. They also run a mentoring programme (371 mentees since 2021).

### Moldovan context

- English proficiency is "Moderate" (EF EPI 2025, 536), Romania "High" (593) ([World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/country-rankings/ef-english-proficiency-index-by-country)). ATIC and UNDP ran an "English for IT" course for 400+ women in 2024 ([UNDP](https://www.undp.org/moldova/press-releases/girls-and-women-moldova-have-better-opportunities-excel-it-thanks-english-it-course)). **[Inference]** Our English-only courses will suit part of this audience, not all of it.
- Women are 43% of Moldova's ICT workforce ([Reasons to be Cheerful](https://reasonstobecheerful.world/moldova-women-in-tech-flourishing/)).

### What to clarify and ask for

**Questions to settle first:**

1. Is our workshop one of the paid workshops? If so, what does it cost attendees, and how many places are there?
2. Will our workshop run in English, and is that clear in the listing?
3. What partner title will we have?
4. How many people do they expect?

**[Inference] What to ask for:**

- Listed as **Education Partner** (or Community Partner) on the unde.io event page, in event announcements and on the screen between sessions
- A dedicated LinkedIn and Instagram post introducing Little Parrot as a partner, like Amdaris got
- A speaker spotlight post for the workshop
- An MC mention at the opening or closing, with our QR code on screen
- Our offer included in any post-event message to attendees, sent by them
- Photos of our workshop, and permission to film and reuse it
- Aggregate attendance numbers afterwards

**[Inference] What to offer:**

- The workshop, with our own travel and accommodation
- An event code giving all attendees free course access for a limited time, with its own UTM link and QR code
- A small number of Open Wings scholarships for attendees (it gives them something to announce, and it fits "Rebuilding the Ladder")
- Our own posts promoting the summit before the event and recapping it afterwards

**Timing:** 18 days to go. Agree this in a short confirmation email this week so they have time to post about us.
