## Verdict

No, not as the title. "Technical skills for product managers" gets ~20 searches/month and no trackable SERP — nobody searches that phrase. Its question variants are all 0/mo and resume-flavoured ("what to list for technical skills product manager"), i.e. the little demand there is comes from job-seekers polishing a CV, not buyers of a course. Your buyers search the identity they want ("technical product manager", 1,900/mo, KD 28) or the format ("technical product manager course/certification", 70/mo each, ~$4.60 CPC — low volume but high commercial intent).

## Recommendations

Title on the identity, subtitle on the audience. e.g. "Technical Product Management: Become the Technical PM" with "for non-technical software product managers" as the subhead. That captures the 1,900 + 390/mo cluster while the qualifier still reassures your ICP (and picks up "non technical product manager", KD 17).
Keep "technical skills for product managers" as an H2/blog article, not the product name — it's free to win (KD 0) and useful for internal linking.
Build the course-page cluster around "technical product manager course", "technical product manager certification", and "how to become a technical product manager" — all KD 23-30, real $4-5 CPC intent, and cheap to rank for.
Supporting lesson pages on the skill sub-terms: "sql for product managers", "apis for product managers" ($11.54 CPC — highest commercial value in the set), "coding for product managers". Low volume individually, but KD 0 and they convert.
Avoid "product management course" (KD 66) and treat "ai for product managers" (1,900/mo, KD 59) as a later expansion module — high demand, needs authority first.
Any of these are still small-volume niches — pair the SEO with paid/LinkedIn, don't expect search alone to fill the cohort.
