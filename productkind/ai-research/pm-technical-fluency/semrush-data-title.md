### Verdict

No, not as the title. "Technical skills for product managers" gets ~20 searches/month and no trackable SERP — nobody searches that phrase. Its question variants are all 0/mo and resume-flavoured ("what to list for technical skills product manager"), i.e. the little demand there is comes from job-seekers polishing a CV, not buyers of a course. Your buyers search the identity they want ("technical product manager", 1,900/mo, KD 28) or the format ("technical product manager course/certification", 70/mo each, ~$4.60 CPC — low volume but high commercial intent).

### Recommendations

Title on the identity, subtitle on the audience. e.g. "Technical Product Management: Become the Technical PM" with "for non-technical software product managers" as the subhead. That captures the 1,900 + 390/mo cluster while the qualifier still reassures your ICP (and picks up "non technical product manager", KD 17).
Keep "technical skills for product managers" as an H2/blog article, not the product name — it's free to win (KD 0) and useful for internal linking.
Build the course-page cluster around "technical product manager course", "technical product manager certification", and "how to become a technical product manager" — all KD 23-30, real $4-5 CPC intent, and cheap to rank for.
Supporting lesson pages on the skill sub-terms: "sql for product managers", "apis for product managers" ($11.54 CPC — highest commercial value in the set), "coding for product managers". Low volume individually, but KD 0 and they convert.
Avoid "product management course" (KD 66) and treat "ai for product managers" (1,900/mo, KD 59) as a later expansion module — high demand, needs authority first.
Any of these are still small-volume niches — pair the SEO with paid/LinkedIn, don't expect search alone to fill the cohort.

### Verdict
"How Software Works for Product Managers" is a weak SEO course title.

### Why the title fails for organic

Nobody is typing that exact phrase — Semrush returns no volume, and there is no SERP to compete for. Even the component parts ("how software works," "software for product managers") pull tiny search volume, and the only sizeable related phrase, "how does software work," is dominated by Wikipedia, Reddit, Stanford, Khan Academy, and Quora answering general curiosity, not a course purchase.

### Performance marketer recommendations

1. Do not lead with this title. Use it as a module name, subtitle, or content hook instead — it describes the value well, but it won't drive discovery.
2. Lead the SEO title with proven demand: "Technical Product Management" / "Technical Product Manager" (1,900/mo, KD 29).
3. Add the non-technical qualifier in the title or subtitle, e.g.:
   - Technical Product Management for Non-Technical PMs
   - How to Think Like a Technical Product Manager (No Coding Background Required)
   - Software Foundations for Product Managers: From User Story to System Design
4. Use the mental-model concept in sales copy, not in the searchable title. "Form a correct mental model of how a digital product works" is a strong value proposition, but buyers search for outcomes and identities ("technical PM," "better engineer collaboration," "pass technical interviews") rather than "mental model."
5. Target the long-tail question keywords in blog/SEO content, not the course title:
   - "Can I be a product manager without technical background" (20/mo)
   - "Do product managers need to be technical" (20/mo)
   - "How technical should a product manager be" (20/mo)
   These are small but high-intent — perfect for articles that funnel into the course.

Bottom line: keep the "how software works" framing for inside the curriculum and the landing page body, but name the course around "Technical Product Management" or "Technical Product Manager" if you want organic findability.
