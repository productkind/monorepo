## Top 5 problems worth a deeper second pass

1. Client reporting automation (Rank 1, score 88). Consultants and small agencies lose a day per month per client assembling reports, and the incumbent fix is a $59–250/mo SaaS subscription — AgencyAnalytics alone pulls 3,600 searches/mo of brand volume and supports a whole "alternatives" ecosystem at KD 13. Advertiser CPCs of $14–24 with low paid density mean money is moving but the keyword space isn't contested. AI changes the economics completely: pulling metrics into a narrative report is exactly what an LLM + a few API connectors does well, so "cancel the subscription, build your own dashboard" is a credible course promise. A two-person team can compete because the SERP is competitor listicles and a Reddit thread — nobody owns the "do it yourself" angle, which is also Little Parrot's home turf.

2. Customer feedback / VoC analysis (Rank 2, score 86). The CPC evidence is the strongest in the entire study: $25–50 across "customer feedback software/platform/tools/survey software" with remarkably low paid density (0.02–0.17), meaning advertisers pay premium prices but haven't saturated these terms. PMs currently pay Dovetail/Canny/Enterpret hundreds per month or burn researcher hours tagging feedback by hand — the problem is weekly and continuous. LLM synthesis of qualitative feedback is arguably the single best-proven AI use case in product work, so the "you don't need the $500/mo tool" story is honest. Competition is listicle blogs and Canny's homepage — a practical workflow course can win the informational layer.

3. Client onboarding & intake (Rank 3, score 85). A big cluster (~6,000/mo) with head-term CPC of $31 (client onboarding) and easy long-tails at KD 16–29 — including lovely niche variants like "bookkeeping client onboarding checklist" (390/mo) that signal per-profession expansion paths. People pay Rocketlane, Clustdoc and Karbon $30–200/mo largely to look professional during week one of a client relationship. AI generates the intake forms, welcome packets, checklists and even a simple portal, collapsing both the SaaS cost and the setup time. The SERP is template blog posts (Zapier, Karbon) plus Reddit — a genuinely useful free template + build-your-own-portal course can take the informational rankings.

4. SaaS SEO for founders (Rank 4, score 84). ~10,000/mo aggregate with agency-grade CPCs ($14–32) — "saas seo agency" alone is 3,600/mo at $27.80 — proving founders routinely pay $2–5k/mo retainers for this. Yet the SERP for "seo for saas" is a Reddit thread at #1 followed by small agency blogs; no HubSpot or Ahrefs wall. A founder who just vibe-coded an app has zero distribution and is the exact "I built it, now what?" persona. AI handles the mechanical 80% of early SEO (keyword clustering, briefs, programmatic pages), which is what makes a course credible as an agency substitute. This also chains naturally onto Little Parrot's existing audience.

5. CRM for consultants (Rank 5, score 82). Small but pristine: ~1,700/mo of pure purchase-intent "crm for consultants/freelancers" at CPCs up to $24.41, KD 16–19, and Reddit holds position #1 — the weakest SERP in the study after client reporting. Consultants pay $15–50/mo for Pipedrive or HubSpot, use 10% of the features, and frequently churn back to spreadsheets. "Build your own lightweight CRM" is a flagship Lovable use case that matches the audience's actual sophistication. A two-person company can own the informational layer because the ranking content is vendor blogs that can't honestly say "maybe you don't need our product."

## The picks

A. Highest willingness-to-pay candidate — Customer feedback / VoC analysis. $49.50 CPC on "customer satisfaction survey software", $34+ on feedback-platform terms, and every incumbent (Dovetail, Canny, Enterpret, Marvin) charges team-tier SaaS pricing. Recurring, budgeted, and demonstrably painful — the strongest money trail in the data.

B. Best small-team opportunity — Client reporting automation. Combines KD 13 entry points, $14–24 CPCs, proven SaaS churn frustration, and a product shape (course + templates + a self-built dashboard) a two-person education company can ship in weeks. Already validated by your earlier deep-dive.

C. Best long-tail search opportunity — Client onboarding & intake. Dozens of specific buyer-intent long-tails (per-profession checklists, intake form templates, process templates) where no single competitor owns the whole problem — the head term is contested by template blogs, but the long tail is wide open and each niche variant (bookkeeping, design, marketing) is its own winnable page.

D. Most surprising opportunity — Client portal software. Wasn't on the seed list; surfaced from the data. ~6,000–8,000/mo, CPCs to $35.69 ("accounting firm management software"), with accountants, lawyers and consultants paying SuiteDash/Copilot $20–100/mo for what is essentially a branded file-and-message hub. "Build your own client portal with AI" is a perfect vibe-coding course premise with a measurable money-saving story.

E. Three attractive-looking markets to reject:
1. Scope creep (9,900/mo) — the volume is real but the intent is definitional ("what is scope creep", $0–2 CPCs, KD 63 head). Nobody searches it with a wallet open; it monetises poorly despite looking like a juicy freelancer-pain keyword.
2. Product management certification (4,400/mo) — looks like education demand, but KD 53, paid density 0.70, and the buyer typically needs employer procurement or a recognised brand (Product School, Reforge own this). A two-person company can't win the credential game, and certificate-buyers aren't course-of-the-month buyers.
3. User onboarding software ($23–72 CPCs) — the CPCs are intoxicating but misleading: the $50+ values are HR/employee-onboarding software (different buyer entirely), and the user-onboarding SERP is locked by product homepages (Pendo, Userflow, Appcues, WalkMe) that a content site can't outrank. Money exists, but not for a course-shaped player.
