# Observed search phrases for non-technical PM technical fluency

**Observed:** 24 August 2026  
**Research window used for result/content checks:** 1 August 2024–24 August 2026  
**Purpose:** exact-query input for a later Semrush stage  
**Semrush used in this stage:** no

## Method and evidence standard

The six need families came from the [validated PM-needs research](./non-technical-pm-technical-needs-validation-2026-08.md). Discovery then used ordinary learner wording rather than requiring “product manager” in the query.

- **94 discovery seeds** were passed through Google autocomplete (English, GB) and YouTube autocomplete (English, GB and US). The working seed set is [here](../marketing/channels/niche-research-tools/pm-technical-fluency-seeds.txt).
- **20 Google result pages** were inspected for top-result intent, People Also Ask and related searches. Google reported that results were not personalised; the browser location was Portugal.
- The top 12 YouTube results were inspected for **13 representative queries** across the six need families.
- Comments were inspected on five representative videos, including current non-technical and PM-specific explainers.
- A phrase appears below only if it was returned verbatim by an autocomplete endpoint, People Also Ask, related searches or a YouTube search interface. Seed phrases that returned no matching observation were not retained.
- Capitalisation is preserved where Google displayed it. Search intent and audience fit are interpretive judgements; the phrase itself is observed evidence.
- Autocomplete proves that a phrase is produced by the interface, not how often it is searched. No demand or volume estimate is made here.
- The final set contains **157 unique observed phrases**; the Semrush shortlist contains **56** of them.

Audience-fit scale:

- **3:** directly relevant to a generalist PM/non-engineer learning the concept;
- **2:** relevant, with substantial developer, student or adjacent-professional intent;
- **1:** primarily developer, engineering-student or specialist intent.

## YouTube result and comment checks

The table's fit judgements were informed by these representative result-page checks:

- **Whole-product/system flow is unusually well matched to non-engineers.** [“Frontend, API, Backend and Database explained”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NzEYYemQ3_8), published 31 October 2024, had 110,924 views on a channel with roughly 2,300 subscribers and 180 comments. Its description explicitly names Product Managers, Product Owners and other non-technical professionals. A commenter called it the explanation needed to understand “the apps architecture”.
- A PM-specific [browser-to-server explainer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k9sTT7R67ik) had 23,223 views and comments asking for “more tech videos for PM”. It predates the research window, so it is used only to validate audience fit, not recency.
- A PM-specific [API explainer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T5y0WKmKenQ) had 69,048 views and 55 comments. Viewers asked for API types, differences, use cases, MQ versus API and more detail on integrations. It also predates the window and is used only as audience-fit evidence.
- **Current non-technical AI-building demand is visible.** [“Full Claude Code Tutorial for Non-Technical Beginners in 2026”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqJzIWAEn40), published 30 April 2026, had 186,657 views and 136 comments at observation. Comments describe previous beginner tutorials as needlessly complex, fear of starting, successful first builds and wanting the steps for launching an app.
- Not every apparently strong view count was meaningful. A December 2025 [PM AI-prototyping video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SBZGZiYFs_k) had 69,917 views but only 44 likes and three low-information comments on a 91,500-subscriber channel. It was not treated as strong demand evidence.
- API explanation results were dense: multiple top videos had more than one million views, including short plain-English explainers and a full beginner course. This indicates a mature, mixed audience rather than PM-exclusive demand.
- CI/CD results were also strong but developer-heavy. A one-month-old five-minute explainer had about 73,000 views, while the top results were predominantly DevOps channels and tutorials.
- Exact YouTube searches for bug reproduction were weak and noisy: results included insects, and the leading software-specific result had under 100 views. Log-reading results skewed towards operating systems, Linux and security.
- Technical-feasibility results were sparse and mostly framed as formal feasibility studies, business projects or coursework. The software-PM motivation is validated elsewhere, but the natural search territory is weak.

## Important gaps

- **Feasibility language is weak.** “How to assess technical feasibility” exists, but results mostly concern formal feasibility studies, small-business projects or coursework—not a PM pressure-testing a software feature.
- **Technical dependencies have poor audience fit.** Observed queries such as “software dependencies meaning” and “software dependencies examples” lead mainly to package/dependency-management content for developers. “Dependencies meaning in project management” is accessible but loses the technical-system context.
- **The end-to-end release need fragments into component searches.** Seeds such as “how does code get to production” and “what happens after code is committed” returned no matching autocomplete phrase. Search behaviour appears to split across CI/CD, pull requests, builds, staging, deployment and feature flags.
- **PM-safe technical investigation is not a clean search territory.** Bug reproduction and triage have useful Google formulations, but YouTube results for the exact bug query are noisy. Log and incident searches skew towards OS administration, security, SRE or ITIL.
- **Prototype-to-production boundaries are under-expressed.** “prototype vs production software” and “vibe coding production apps” exist, but most AI-building searches focus on getting a first app working, not security, correctness, maintenance or engineering handoff.
- **Product-specific mental models will rarely surface as generic search demand.** A PM normally needs to understand their own company's services, data and constraints; public search can reveal the conceptual scaffolding but not the internal system question.
- Absence or weak audience fit here does not invalidate the PM need. It means the need may be expressed through colleague questions, internal documentation, courses or adjacent component searches rather than one public keyword.

## Boundaries for the Semrush stage

- Keep the exact observed phrase as the seed; do not prepend “product manager” automatically.
- Compare broad explanation terms with their beginner/simple/non-technical variants rather than treating them as interchangeable.
- Preserve tool-specific AI terms separately from the durable need (“build a bounded working prototype”).

- Do not interpret zero or low volume for feasibility, dependencies or issue investigation as evidence that PMs do not experience those needs.
