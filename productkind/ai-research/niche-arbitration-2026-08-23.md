# Niche arbitration: four research passes, one buyer, one recommendation

**Date:** 23 August 2026
**Question asked:** three documents now recommend different niches for Little Parrot. Which one, and why?
**Status:** a recommendation with a named order of operations. Still no measured purchase anywhere in
this body of work, and that remains the largest gap.

**What this settles.** `high-value-problem-niche-2026-08-23.md` recommended the woman whose AI-built app
carries real customer data. `problem-niche-discovery-2026-08-23.md` recommended sole-practitioner
interior designers first. The two new Semrush pulls,
`semrush-niche-discovery-15-problem-clusters-2026-08.md` and
`semrush-niche-research-compare-five-problem-clusters-2026-08.md`, rank a different cluster first again,
and they also disagree with each other. This document reconciles all four and names one call.

**Verbatim note.** Quotes are reproduced exactly as written. Per the folder convention, any

## 1. The convergence nobody has named yet

Each pass named a *problem*. Nobody has named the *person* sitting underneath all of them, and once you
do, four independent research passes turn out to agree.

| Pass | What it ranked first or near-first |
|---|---|
| 15-cluster Semrush discovery | #1 client reporting automation (88), #3 client onboarding (85), #5 CRM for consultants (82), #7 client portal (80) |
| 5-cluster Semrush comparison | #1 client operations for solo consultants, #2 client reporting automation, and the note that these two are "the same buyer at two different workflow steps" |
| Problem-first discovery | #2 independent service providers on the monthly client deliverable, plus landscape rows 2, 5, 7, 17 and 18 |
| Four-part community study | The build-instead-of-buy move stated with amounts by solo operators: $400 a year of hosting removed, $1,000 a month of tools replaced |

Four of the seven highest-scoring clusters in the first pull are one person. The second pull says so
directly. The community study supplies her motive in her own words.

**She is a solo service provider with a client roster, and the thing that hurts is the client-facing
document she has to produce on a date, per client, forever.**

Two consequences fall out immediately.

**Interior designers are a vertical of this niche, not a rival to it.** A sole practitioner running
three to eight concurrent projects, whose FF&E schedule is the document the client signs off, is the
same person with the same job. The discovery document's first and second recommendations are one niche
and one segment of it, which is why they never resolved against each other.

**The 5 to 15 client band is the trigger, not a descriptor.** Per-client software pricing punishes
exactly that band: AgencyAnalytics at $20 to $25 per client per month gets more expensive as the margin
per client thins. Below five clients she assembles it by hand and it fits in a day. Above fifteen she
hires. The pain has a threshold, and a threshold is a purchase moment.

## 2. What the new pulls change, including where they contradict each other

**The demand gate has moved by two orders of magnitude.** The client-operations cluster is about 19,500
a month in the US plus 5,000 in the UK, of which roughly 16,300 is commercial or transactional, the
highest commercial share of any cluster measured at 84%. Client reporting adds about 13,700 US at 98%
commercial. The safety niche recommended in the sister document is 140 to 170 a month. That is not a
close comparison, and the earlier recommendation should not be defended past it.

**The entry points are unusually cheap for money this warm.** `client dashboard software` at difficulty
7 with an $18.46 cost per click. `seo client reporting` at difficulty 7 on 1,000 a month.
`consulting crm software` at difficulty 14 and $19.46. `best crm for consultants` at difficulty 15 and
$30.65. `client onboarding checklist` at difficulty 16. `client onboarding software` carries $39.21,
the highest cost per click in either pull. Reddit sits at position one or two on several of them, and
solo-consultant blogs rank on others, which means individuals are visibly the buyers and no content
wall exists.

**The two new documents disagree, and the later one wins.** The 15-cluster pull named customer feedback
and voice-of-customer analysis its "highest willingness-to-pay candidate" on $25 to $50 costs per
click. The deeper five-cluster pass then found that the money terms in that cluster are enterprise
voice-of-customer suites, with Qualtrics, Medallia, Salesforce, InMoment and Glassbox holding the
results page, and demoted it to fourth. **Pick A of the first document should not be acted on.** The
same correction applies more mildly to SaaS SEO: its highest costs per click, $32 to $39, are companies
hiring an agency, not an individual buying education.

**And the vibe-coded-builder audience was measured as the weakest commercial pool of the five.** Lowest
commercial share at about 50%, lowest median cost per click at $6.40. The five-cluster document's own
verdict: "Best used as top-of-funnel content feeding a paid product, not as the product's core keyword
set." That is the same conclusion the funnel table in the sister document reached from community
evidence, arrived at independently from search data. It is now confirmed twice, and it is the single
most important correction to Little Parrot's current shelf, because the current shelf is built almost
entirely on that audience.

## 3. The recommendation

> ### The niche
>
> **A solo service provider with five to fifteen retained clients, on the client-facing document she
> has to produce on a fixed date for every one of them.**
>
> ### The positioning
>
> Not "learn to build with AI". **"The document your client signs off, produced without your weekend."**

Against the four tests:

**Specific person.** Self-employed social media and marketing freelancers, one-person creative studios,
sole-practitioner interior designers, solo consultants and bookkeepers. Retainers of $300 to $3,500 a
client. Predominantly women without anyone having to target for it: 84% of US interior designers, and
the social and content segment likewise.

**Painful recurring job.** Assemble numbers from platforms that disagree with each other, drop them into
a template, write the meaning in prose, brand it, send it. One hour per client per month, so five to
fifteen hours, on a fixed date, forever. Verbatim: *"monthly client reporting is one of the biggest time
drains going"*, *"about an hour per client"*, *"Lots of screenshots. Little text."*

**Valuable outcome.** The retainer renews. $300 to $1,650 a month of billable time recovered at the
$60 to $110 experienced-freelance rate, and the larger cost nobody prices, which is a client lost
because the value was never legible.

**AI dramatically changes how it is done.** This is the strongest of the four legs. Pull the data,
write the interpretation in her voice against her own client context, render it in her brand, on a
schedule. Every step of that is a proven strength rather than a hope.

### What it passes that the earlier recommendation failed

| Gate | Client deliverable | App safety (earlier pick) |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial search volume | ~16,300/mo US commercial, 84% share | 140 to 170/mo |
| Advertiser money proven | $14 to $39 per click, verified density | No measured density |

| Little Parrot format fit | Scored highest of all five clusters at 4.8 | 4.1 |
| Reachability | Best in the research, and the audience most likely to reshare a free link | Medium |

## 4. The one real problem with it, and how to design around it

**The intent behind that money is to buy a different product, or to grab a template. It is not to learn
to build.** This is measured, not suspected. `automate client reporting` returned zero.
`client reporting tool for freelancers`, `hootsuite alternative for freelancers` and
`client dashboard for freelancers` returned no data at all. `how to automate client onboarding` is 20 a
month. Meanwhile every phrase describing what Little Parrot actually teaches returned zero or no data,
and `build my own crm` carries the highest cost per click in the whole pull, $29.31, on twenty searches
a month.

A person clicking `agencyanalytics alternative` at $14 to $28 has a card out and is comparing software.
Selling her a course is an interception.

Three moves make it a match rather than an interception, and all three are cheap.

**One: name every page after the document or the tool she is leaving. Never after the build.** The
measured vocabulary is the incumbent's name plus "alternative", the word "template", or the tool's
name. So: "Client report template", "AgencyAnalytics alternatives, honestly compared", "FF&E schedule
template". The build is what happens inside, and she finds out after she arrives.

**Two: the template is the free front door, and it is the same acquisition mechanic Little Parrot
already runs.** The searchable paid-for demand is for finished artefacts. That is not a defeat of the
premise, it is a route into it: the template ranks and gets shared, and the course is sold to the woman
who outgrows it. Paid users sharing free links is already the primary acquisition channel, so this
costs nothing to adopt.

**Three: the course promise is the one thing a template cannot do, which is regenerate itself.** A
template is static and her data changed. The $20 tool assembles the numbers and will not write the
meaning. Neither produces next month's document. That gap is the entire paid promise, and interpreting
numbers into a client-facing recommendation is product management work, which is Kinga's own expertise
rather than a claim we would have to build.

**And the comparison page has to be honest or it will bounce.** Name the real products first, say
plainly who should just buy AgencyAnalytics, and offer building last. Anything else fails against a
buyer who is mid-comparison.

## 5. Where the app-safety niche goes now

It stops being a competing niche and becomes the retention half of this one, which is a stronger
position than either document gave it.

The sequence is causal rather than adjacent. She buys the client-report course. She now has a tool with
her clients' data in it that they depend on every month. **The safety problem is now hers, and it
arrived because of the first purchase.** It is the only candidate in the research where the job returns
with every change, which is the shape a EUR 24 a month subscription needs, and the free competitor has
just lost the admin who owned the topic (*"I'm stepping down from admin duties... hopefully someone
will take over"*).

So: not the acquisition niche, on measured demand. The second and third courses on the ladder, and the
reason the subscription renews.

## 6. What this does to the existing shelf

It reframes it rather than replacing it. Nine courses currently sit on the vibe-coded-builder audience,
which both new pulls measured as the weakest commercial pool. They are not wasted, they are the
mechanism rather than the offer.

*Build Your First App with Lovable*, *Fix Bugs with Confidence*, *Save Lovable Credits* and *Launch and
Grow Your Lovable App* become the how, taught inside a course whose promise is the client document. The
Lovable comparison cluster keeps its own search value independently, at roughly 3,000 a month in the US
at difficulty 13 to 27 from the earlier production-ready pull.

**The ladder:**

1. **Free template**, per vertical. Ranks, gets shared, gives her the artefact today.
2. **The document that produces itself.** Her data, her voice, her brand, on the same date every month.
3. **The safe version.** A release check, a change she can undo, a rehearsed restore, now that clients depend on it.
4. **The next document.** Intake and scoping, then retainer profitability. Same buyer, previous and next workflow step, which is exactly what the five-cluster comparison found: client onboarding is about 6,000 a month with a per-profession long tail where each variant is its own winnable page.

## 7. The arithmetic, stated before anyone is surprised

At EUR 24 a month, EUR 5,000 needs about 208 subscribers. Ranking top three on a cluster gives 20 to
30% click-through, and cold organic to paid subscription converts at 1 to 3%, which is generous.

The client-reporting cluster at about 2,800 a month in the US yields roughly 560 to 840 visits, and at
the low end of conversion because the intent is to buy software, so perhaps 6 to 17 subscribers a
month. The interior design cluster yields 3 to 12. Neither reaches the target from search alone inside
a year.

**Search is a compounding asset here, not a launch channel.** The share-link mechanic remains the
faster half of acquisition. What this document changes is which door the search half knocks on.

## 8. Order of operations

**Step zero, and it costs nothing.** One hour inside one private interior design Facebook group,
searching FF&E, schedule, spreadsheet and Programa, or one conversation with a working sole
practitioner. The interior design cluster has the best measured door in all the research at difficulty
7 and advertiser density 0.99, and the worst pain evidence: none first-person. This decides whether the
first vertical is interior designers or social media managers, where the pain is already evidenced
verbatim. Needs Kinga's decision, because joining is an action on her account.

**Then, in order:**

1. **One free template plus one course behind it**, in whichever vertical step zero picks. Success: the
   template ranks top three within twelve weeks and 2% or better of downloaders subscribe. Failure: it
   ranks and nobody subscribes, which would mean this is a template market rather than an education
   market. That is a real possible outcome and it is worth knowing for the price of one template.
2. **The honest comparison page** on AgencyAnalytics alternatives, with about GBP 300 of paid search on
   the cluster. This answers the only open question in the niche, which is whether a switcher with a
   card out will buy education instead. Success: visitors who state five or more clients subscribe at 2%
   or better.
3. **The safety course**, sold to whoever completed step one, as the retention test rather than an
   acquisition test.

**Rejected, and recorded so they are not rediscovered:** customer feedback and voice-of-customer
analysis, on the enterprise results-page wall despite the highest costs per click in either pull; SaaS
SEO as a paid niche, because its expensive half is companies hiring agencies; the broad AI-consulting
demand, which the community votes ranked first at 40% but which has no search channel and a free
competitor serving it now; and the vibe-coded-builder cluster as a *headline* niche, which both pulls
independently measured as the weakest commercial pool while confirming its value as top-of-funnel
content.

## 9. What is still not known

- **No purchase has been measured anywhere.** Every recommendation in every one of these documents is a
  hypothesis. Step one above is the first instrument that produces a real answer.
- **Single-source volume data**, since Keyword Planner is still blocked.
- **The interior design pain is unverified**, and it is the first recommendation's whole risk.
- **No supply audit of competing courses per vertical.** This research has twice killed a
  recommendation on finding that the incumbent gives it away, so the shelf should be inventoried before
  committing.
- **Gumroad and Etsy were never mined.** Template sales are the closest thing to behavioural evidence
  available without talking to anyone, and this niche now rests on templates being the front door.
