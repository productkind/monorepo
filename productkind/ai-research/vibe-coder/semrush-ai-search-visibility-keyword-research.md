# AI Search Visibility — Keyword Research

Source: Semrush (Google organic, US and UK databases). Checked 30 Sep 2026. Companion to `schema-markup-keyword-research.md` — this file covers the "how do I get seen in search and AI search" angle for the SEO article.

Volumes are Semrush estimates, not measured visits. "Difficulty" estimates competition; a low score is encouraging evidence, not a promise of rankings. Global volume isn't available through these reports — US and UK are shown, and the US figure is the best proxy for overall demand.

"—" means Semrush returned no data: demand is unknown, not necessarily zero. For emerging terms like these, "—" often means "so new it isn't tracked yet", not "nobody searches it" — people clearly do phrase things this way (see the paid-ad competition on the tracked variants).

## 5. What this means for the article

- Primary target: **"how to get my website to show up on google"** (590 US, difficulty 20) — the classic search-visibility question with the best volume-to-difficulty ratio.
- Secondary/section targets: the ChatGPT business questions (difficulty 0, CPC $13–16) — give each its own FAQ question so it can win the answer box and AI-citation slot.
- Pain-point phrasings ("why is my website not showing on google", "chatgpt wrong information about my business") make empathetic H2s that match how non-technical readers actually search.
- Keep head terms like "ai search optimization" and "ai visibility" in the copy once for context; don't build the page around them (difficulty 51, established competitors).
- Combine with the schema-markup research: the FAQ questions from both files can ship as FAQPage structured data, tying the article's topic (being understood by machines) to its own implementation.
