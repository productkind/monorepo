## Is Your MVP Small Enough?

A checklist for your MVP (Minimum Viable Product): the smallest version of your product that's still useful for your target user. Go through this while or after you've sliced out your MVP on your story map and before you write a single prompt. It helps you to confirm you're building enough to be valuable, but not too much to lose focus.

### Does your MVP solve the core problem?

- [ ] **Can you trace every feature back to your problem statement?** If a feature doesn't help solve the problem you defined in your problem statement, it's not MVP. Save it for later.
- [ ] **Does it address your user's biggest frustration?** Look at your persona, the profile of your target user, and the frustrations you listed. The MVP should tackle at least the top one.
- [ ] **Would your persona find this version useful, even without the extra features?** If Sarah could use the Book Club Organiser to gather every suggestion in one organised place, that's already useful. She doesn't need notifications, profiles, or reading progress tracking yet.

### Is the scope small enough?

- [ ] **Can you describe your MVP in one sentence?** If you need a paragraph, it's too big. For example: "A web app where book club members suggest books and vote on which to read next."
- [ ] **Does your MVP cover only 1-3 activities from your story map?** Most MVPs need the core workflow, not every activity you mapped out.
- [ ] **Have you cut the nice-to-haves?** Go through your details one by one and ask: "Would the core experience still work without this?" If yes, cut it. You can always add it later.
- [ ] **Could you build and test this within a week?** If the scope feels like it would take much longer, you're probably including too much.

### Does it deliver value on its own?

- [ ] **Is it something worth using?** Your MVP doesn't have to cover the whole journey. If the Book Club Organiser only collected suggestions in one organised place, Sarah would already be better off than scrolling back through WhatsApp for a title someone mentioned last week.
- [ ] **Where your app stops, can your user carry on the way they do today?** Handing the rest of the journey back to the tools they already use keeps your first version small. Sarah's MVP includes voting, and an even smaller one could leave the club voting in the group chat while she gets feedback on the suggestions list.
- [ ] **Does the slice you did build work properly?** Cutting scope means fewer features, and each one still needs to work. If Sarah adds a book suggestion, it should save and still be there tomorrow.

### Is it ready for feedback?

- [ ] **Could you show this to a real user this week?** If you're thinking "I need to add one more thing first", that's a sign you're trying to build too much.
- [ ] **Will you learn something from user reactions?** The MVP should test your core assumption about what the user needs. If the feedback would be the same regardless of how you build it, you're not testing anything yet.

### Quick comparison: MVP vs too much

At each new feature you add to your product, first, you can build its MVP version, test it, then build on top of that.

| MVP | Too much |
|---|---|
| Create club with club name | Create club with description, rules, reading pace, and club type |
| Suggest books with title and author | Suggest books with cover image, recommendation note, genre tags, and reading level |
| Vote on books (cast vote, view counts) | Vote with comments, deadline reminders, notification emails, and weighted voting |

The left column solves the problem. The right column is what you build once you've confirmed the left column works.
