## Sketch Your User's Journey

A three-part framework for drawing your user's journey: the moment the problem happens, the moment it's solved, and the steps your product adds in between. Grab a piece of paper or open a drawing app, and sketch one panel at a time.

### The framework

Your storyboard has three parts, in this order:

| Panel | What to draw | Guiding question |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The Struggle** | Your user in the moment they experience the problem. Show the frustration, the mess, the wasted effort. | What is your user doing when the problem hits, and how does it make them feel? |
| **2. The Happy Ending** | Your user after the problem is solved. Show what they are doing (e.g. relaxing, being productive, celebrating). | What can your user do (or stop doing) once the problem is gone? |
| **3-6. The Moments In Between** | The key steps between those two panels. This is where your product appears. | What needs to happen, step by step, for your user to reach the happy ending? |

### Why draw the struggle and the happy ending first?

Knowing where your user starts and where they need to end up makes the middle much easier to work out. You're connecting two points you already understand, rather than inventing features from nothing.

### Filled-in example: Book Club Organiser

**Panel 1: The Struggle**

Sarah is at the kitchen table, scrolling through endless WhatsApp messages, trying to find a book title someone suggested last week. She looks overwhelmed.

![Storyboard panel one: Sarah at her kitchen table, scrolling through a long stream of WhatsApp messages, looking overwhelmed as she searches for a book title](/courses/vibe-coding-product-management-00/1-the-struggle.png)

**Panel 2: The Happy Ending**

Sarah is relaxing on the sofa with a book and a glass of wine. The book club coordination happened without effort and she has time to enjoy reading.

![Storyboard panel two: Sarah relaxed on the sofa with a book and a glass of wine, the book club sorted](/courses/vibe-coding-product-management-00/2-the-happy-ending.png)

**Panels 3-6: The Moments In Between**

3. Sarah discovers the Book Club Organiser app and creates her book club in it.
4. Sarah and her friends add book suggestions and browse what everyone has recommended.
5. The group votes on their favourites.
6. The app announces the winning book.

![Storyboard panels three to six: Sarah finds the app and creates her book club, she and her friends add book suggestions, the group votes, and the app announces the next book to read](/courses/vibe-coding-product-management-00/3-6-discovery-to-decision.png)

### Tips for sketching

- **Stick figures are perfect.** You're capturing the moment and the feeling, and stick figures do that well.
- **One moment per panel.** Each panel should show one clear scene. If you're trying to fit two things in, split it into two panels.
- **Show emotions.** A frowning face in the struggle panel and a smiling face in the happy ending tell the story at a glance.
- **Write a caption under each panel.** A sentence or two explaining what's happening. The caption is as useful as the drawing.
- **Keep it to 5-7 panels.** Enough to tell the story, few enough that it stays manageable.

### After your storyboard

Your storyboard becomes the foundation for your user story map. The "moments in between" panels map directly to the activities and steps you'll break down.
