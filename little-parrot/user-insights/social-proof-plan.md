# Social proof plan for littleparrot.app

How to use the 11 known LinkedIn posts, plus the in-app feedback, to strengthen social proof on the site.

## What is on the site today

| Block | What it proves | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| "Built by product builders previously at" + Datadog, Citrix, Volkswagen, Cloudera | Kinga and Thomas can build | Proves the founders are credible, not that the visitor can build |
| "What Our Learners Say" + 3 quotes (Sesha Kadakia, Jane Tonal, Michelle Grandville) | The courses are well made | Initials-only avatars, no link, no date, unverifiable. Reads as the default template component |
| No numbers anywhere | | |

The visitor's real doubt is not "is this course well made?" It is "can someone like me build a working thing, in the time I have?" Praise answers the first question. Shipped apps answer the second, and the LinkedIn posts are full of them.

## Priority order

### 2. Leave the existing three quotes broadly as they are

Sesha Kadakia, Jane Tonal and Michelle Grandville were each asked before their quote, full name and title went up, so nothing needs re-clearing.

The "make them verifiable" upgrade also mostly does not apply to these three, because their quotes did not come from public posts:
- **Sesha's** is a tidied version of her in-app completion feedback for Basics of Software for Vibe Coding (9 December 2025).
- **Michelle's** is from her Write Better With AI completion feedback (8 October 2025).
- **Jane's** reads like direct feedback too. Her LinkedIn post is in French and does not contain that quote, so the post is a separate, additional piece of proof rather than the source of the one on the site.

So there is no post to link for Sesha or Michelle, and initials are honest for quotes that were given privately. Keep them as they are. If a photo would be nice to have, fold the ask into the next natural contact rather than making a special request; the credibility gain from a photo is small next to the gain from the linked cards in section 1.

What is still worth adding to this section:
- **Grace Hall** and **Elena Martin Hernandez** as two new quote cards, both public and therefore linked. Grace does specific work here: a lead full stack engineer vouching for the product thinking answers the "is this actually rigorous?" objection that praise from beginners cannot.
- One honest line under the section heading: **"Nobody here was paid to write this."** Jane Tonal wrote exactly that in her own post, so it is a fact about the set, not a claim invented for the site.

Light tidying of quotes for grammar is already the house practice. Keep doing that, and never rewrite a quote into house voice. Verbatim quotes are exempt from the banned-word list; the surrounding copy is not.

### 3. Add a compact mentions strip, not a wall of eleven cards

Below the quotes: a dense row of name + role chips, each linking to the post. It shows breadth cheaply, and the breadth is the point. The posts come from a recruiter, several product managers, a lead engineer, an HR and DEI curator, an internal comms manager and a French-speaking coach. That range signals "not one niche" better than any single quote.

Include: Ramona Maniceanu (Senior TA / Sourcing Specialist), Chloe Falcon and Claire Ma at Staffbase, Marisha Drayton (CoolJobs), Jane Tonal, Trinica A., Sophie Wright, Grace Hall, Elena Martin Hernandez.

Claire Ma's line is worth pulling out in full because the disclaimer does the persuading: "this cute little vibe coding course is too cool not to share... (btw this isn't an ad!! i just wanted to share the joy of finding cool, free courses online)".

### 4. Put proof where the decision happens

All the proof currently sits in one block, mid-homepage. Spread it:

- **Hero, one line under Start Learning.** The strongest single outcome: Sophie designed, built and published an app prototype in under an hour.
- **Every course page, one quote from that course's own feedback.** This is the biggest untapped asset in the repo, because files `02`–`09` are already split by course:
  - Basics of Software for Vibe Coding → Sesha: "Fantastic course, may be the best I've seen so far for breaking down core software concepts!"
  - Build Your First App with Lovable → Lindsay: "I made and published an app that I will use everyday", or Marie: "a very well designed course, it really did feel like a calm way to learn"
  - Build a Valuable Product → Grace Hall's toolkit post, plus Sesha: "I am an overthinker so the AI feedback was great"
  - Write Better With AI → Michelle Grandville (currently on the homepage, move or copy it here)
  - The remaining courses: pull from files `03`–`09`.
- **Pricing page.** One paid-learner quote, plus the scholarship sponsorship fact. Marisha's IWD post praises the pricing copy itself ("The descriptions behind these price points are chef's kiss") which is unusual and specific enough to quote there.
- **Course completion screen.** A share-ask pointing at the wall page (below), since paid users sharing free-course links is the main acquisition channel.

### 5. Numbers, only the ones that hold up

Do **not** publish the completion-feedback recommendation split. It is 14 yes, 6 maybe, 0 no out of 25 submissions, and while "nobody said they would not recommend it" is true, the base is small enough that a sharp reader will discount it and the surrounding proof with it.

Pull instead from the database, verified fresh before publishing:
- Learners who have started a challenge. The June 2026 extract found 555 distinct learners in the step-response data, so "over 500" is likely conservative, but check it against current numbers rather than reusing that figure.
- Courses live (8).
- Typical challenge length, since "fits into a busy life" is a core promise and a number makes it concrete.

The "only 20% of vibe coders are women" stat works as context for the mission, not as proof of the product. Keep those two jobs separate.

### 6. Build the collection loop

Proof is a stock that decays, and 8 of the 11 posts cluster around International Women's Day in March 2026. In July they still read as real, but the set needs topping up.

- Add a `/wall` page collecting every post in full. It gives the homepage somewhere to link, gives Kinga something to share from her own account, and gives future posters a reason to post.
- When someone posts, DM thanks, ask permission to feature them, and send the wall link. Being featured produces second posts.
- Plan a collection push at the next moment. SheBuilds S3 is already in the repo at `productkind/marketing/content/campaigns/2026-06-shebuilds-s03`.
- Do not date the cards. The link carries the date for anyone who wants to check, and an undated card does not go stale on the page.

### 7. Consent

Quoting a public LinkedIn post with the person's name, role and a link back is normal practice and what the platform is for. Profile photos are different: ask before using one. Asking is also the growth move, because the reply is usually another post.

## Design notes

- The outcome cards become the most prominent proof block. Move the founder logo bar **below** them: "she shipped it" outranks "we worked at Datadog" for this visitor, and the logo bar currently occupies the stronger position.
- Give each card one focal figure (the build time, in tabular numerals) so the section survives the squint test. The quote is supporting text, not the headline.
- One accent colour for every outbound post link, used nowhere else, so "this is checkable" becomes a learned pattern within the section.
- Four cards, not eleven. The mentions strip carries volume; the cards carry weight.

## Proposed section copy

Headings and framing, written for the site. Quotes stay verbatim.

- Quote section heading: **In their own words**
- Quote section note: **Nobody here was paid to write this.**
