# Paid user interview pack, September 2026

**Purpose:** understand why 5 of our 7 paid users left and what the 2 who stayed have in common, before we decide which niche and which learning path to build.

**Why now:** `productkind/marketing/positioning-2026-08.md` section 4 ranked this the highest-value research available, at no cost and blocking nothing. It has not been run. Both validation campaigns (technical Product Manager, vibe-coded app) are waiting on a decision these seven conversations can inform.

**Population:** every person who has ever paid. 7 people, 5 churned, 2 active. This is not a sample, it is the census. Aim for all seven.

**Format:** 25 minutes, video call, recorded with permission. Verbatim notes into this folder, one file per participant.

---

## 1. Ground rules

These matter more than the question list.

- **This is not a win-back call.** Do not pitch, do not offer a discount, do not defend a course. The moment they sense a save attempt, you stop getting the truth. Say so in the first thirty seconds.
- **Thomas should run the churn interviews where possible.** People soften criticism when they are talking to the person who wrote the thing. If Kinga runs them, say explicitly that blunt answers are the useful ones.
- **Do not name our hypotheses.** We believe the problems are Lovable credits, a library with no reason to renew, and an ask that lands after the emotional peak. If we say any of that first, all seven will agree with us and we will learn nothing. Let them raise it. If nobody has by the end, use the direct probes in section 5, last.
- **Ask what happened, not what would happen.** "Walk me through the day you cancelled" beats "why did you cancel". "What have you paid for since" beats "would you pay for this".
- **Ask for the most recent time, not the usual time.** Generalisations are reconstructions; incidents are memories.
- **Leave silence after an answer.** The second thing they say is usually the real one.
- **Follow their words.** If they say "the app thing", say "the app thing". Do not upgrade their vocabulary to ours.

**Before you call:** pull what you already have on each person. Signup date, cancellation date, which courses they opened and how far they got, whether they ever completed one, and any support or Discord messages. Reconstruct the timeline first so you can ask about specific weeks rather than asking them to remember for you.

## 2. The invitation

**To the five who left:**

> Hi [name], you subscribed to Little Parrot earlier this year and then cancelled. I'm trying to understand what we got wrong, and you are one of a very small number of people who can tell me. Could I have 25 minutes? I'm not going to try to sell you anything or win you back, and it would genuinely help if you were blunt. Happy to keep anything you say anonymous.

**To the two who stayed:**

> Hi [name], you are one of a handful of people paying for Little Parrot, so your view carries a lot of weight in what we build next. Could I have 25 minutes to hear what you are actually using it for and what is missing? Not a sales call, and no upgrade attached.

---

## 3. Guide A: the five who left

### Before they paid

1. Take me back to before you subscribed. What were you building, and where were you stuck?
2. How did you first come across Little Parrot, and what was the first thing you did on the site?
3. What was happening on the day you paid? What had you just done, or just tried to do and failed at?
4. Was there something specific you expected to get by paying? What made that worth paying for rather than finding it free, or asking an AI chat assistant?
5. What else were you paying for at that point, for the same project?

### What they actually did

6. Walk me through what you did in the first week after paying.
7. Which course or step did you get furthest into? What did you do with it on your own project afterwards?
8. Was there a point where you stopped opening it? What was going on with the project that week?
9. When you got stuck between sessions, where did you actually go?

### The cancellation

10. Take me to the day you cancelled. What were you doing just before you did it?
11. How long had you been thinking about it before you clicked?
12. Had anything changed in the weeks before: the project, your budget, your time, your job?
13. Was there something you were waiting for that never arrived?
14. Is there anything that annoyed you that you have not mentioned? Small things count.

### Where they are now

15. Where is the project today? Is it live, does anyone use it, has anyone paid for it?
16. What is standing between it and real customers right now?
17. What have you paid for since, related to any of this? Tools, courses, a person, anything.
18. Who do you go to now when you get stuck?

### Close

19. If you were us and could build one thing next, what would it be and who would it be for?
20. What should I have asked you?

---

## 4. Guide B: the two who stayed

### What keeps it

1. What did you open most recently, and what were you trying to do?
2. What are you using the subscription for at the moment?
3. Think about the last payment that went out. What had it been worth to you that month?
4. Have you ever come close to cancelling? When, and what stopped you?
5. What would make you cancel?

### What it replaced

6. What were you doing about this before you paid us?
7. What else are you paying for on the same project? How do you think about ours next to those?
8. Has anything you learned here saved you money or time you can point at?

### The project

9. Where is your project now, and what have you shipped since you subscribed?
10. What is the next thing you need to work out, and do you expect to find it with us?

### Positioning in their words

11. Have you recommended Little Parrot to anyone? What did you tell them it was?
12. If a friend asked what it is for, what would you say?
13. Who do you think it is not for?

### Close

14. If we could only build one thing next, what should it be?
15. What should I have asked you?

---

## 5. Direct probes, last resort only

Use these only in the final three minutes, and only if the topic has not come up on its own. Record that you prompted it, because a prompted answer is much weaker evidence than a volunteered one.

- Was the price a factor? What would it have needed to be? _(For the churned: only after they have given their own account.)_
- Did Lovable credits or Lovable's own cost affect how you used the subscription?
- Did you ever run out of things you wanted to do in the courses?
- When you paid, were you expecting a set of courses or a path to a specific result?

---

## 6. What to record

One file per participant, verbatim where you can. Then across all seven, answer these:

1. **Rejection or drift?** For each of the five, was cancelling a considered decision about value, or an admin tidy-up after they had already stopped using it? These need different fixes and we cannot tell them apart from the Stripe data.
2. **What do the two who stayed share?** Situation, project stage, role, what they were paying us instead of. If it is one thing, that is the closest evidence we have to a niche.
3. **Which path were they on?** Does each of the seven sit closer to the technical Product Manager position or the vibe-coded-app position? Seven data points will not settle the niche question, but they are the only paying evidence we have on either.
4. **What did each of them pay for after leaving?** This is the strongest willingness-to-pay signal available to us, and it costs one question.
5. **Their words.** Collect the phrases they use for the problem and for what we are. Those go straight into landing page copy.

Do this before briefing anyone external on the niche question.
