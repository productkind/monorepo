# The follow-up: two research questions

Send these **after** they say yes, in the same thread. Not bundled into the first message: a permission ask is a two-second yes, and two open questions turn it into homework.

## Why these two questions

The in-course data stops at completion. Across ~1,155 in-course reflections and 25 completion forms, there is almost nothing about what happened afterwards, so **"what did you do next" is the real gap** and the more valuable of the two questions.

Expect the second question to return credits and Lovable pricing. That is already the No. 1 blocker in the data with 35+ mentions, and cost opacity is second. If all four say credits, that is confirmation rather than new information. The new information will be in what they hit *after* the course ended.

Two changes to the phrasing, both from your own findings:

- **"Was there a point where you felt stuck or wanted more?"** invites "yes" and nothing else, and it asks two things at once. Ask **where** instead of **whether**, which presupposes there was a point and makes the honest answer the easy one.
- The synthesis found open-recall prompts yield one-word answers while scaffolded ones produced the deepest responses in the whole dataset. So "what did you do next" gets three options attached, including permission to say "I parked it", which is the answer you most need and are least likely to get unprompted.

## Where the answers go

Add a new file, `10-alumni-follow-up.md`, following the same verbatim convention as `01`–`09`: reproduce exactly what they wrote, attribute as recorded, and note the date. Four answers is a small sample, so treat it as direction to investigate rather than evidence, and fold anything that recurs into `00-insights-synthesis.md` with the source named.

If the "what next" answers turn out interesting, this is worth turning into a standing habit at course completion rather than a one-off, since the same question asked of everyone would fill the biggest hole in the dataset.
